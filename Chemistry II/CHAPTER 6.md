# Introduction to Organic Chemistry

> [!definition]- Organic Chemistry
> Organic chemistry is a branch of chemistry that focuses on compounds that contain carbon
> > [!info]- Exception
> > $\ce{CO}$, $\ce{CO_{2}}$, carbonates and carbides
> 
> > [!continue]- Speciality
> > Even though organic compounds only contain a few elements, the unique way carbon atoms can attach together to form molecules leads to millions of different organic compounds

> [!liststart]- Characteristics
> > [!continue]- Mainly composed of just six elements
> > $\ce{C}$, $\ce{H}$, $\ce{O}$, $\ce{N}$, $\ce{S}$ and $\ce{P}$
> 
> > [!continue]- If contain halides group elements, called:
> > organohalides
> 
> > [!continue]- Compounds found in ||all three states||
> > solids (tend to have ||low|| melting point), liquids and gases
> 
> > [!continue]- Solubility in water varies depending on
> > which elements are attached to $\ce{C}$ and how many there are
> > > [!example]- 
> > >  - $\ce{CH_{3}OH}$ is ||miscible with water||
> > >  - $\ce{C_{10}H_{21}OH}$ is ||insoluble||

## Homologous Series and General Formula

> [!definition]- Homologous Series
> A series of organic compounds with ||similar chemical properties||, in which each member differs from the previous one by the addition of a ||$\ce{-CH2-}$ group||
> > [!info]- Homologues
> > The members of a homologous series are called homologues

> [!liststart]- General Properties
> > [!continue]- Same ||functional groups|| and similar ||chemical properties||
> 
> > [!continue]- Each member differs from the next in the series by a ||$\ce{-CH2-}$ group||
> 
> > [!continue]- Can be prepared by ||similar methods||
> 
> > [!continue]- Physical properties
> > There is a ||progressive change|| with increasing molecular mass
> 
> > [!continue]- Composition
> > Contain the same elements and are represented by the ||same general formula||

## General Formula for Homologous Series

> [!definition]- General Formula
> A formula that can apply not only to a specific compound but to ||all compounds|| in the same homologous series

> [!info]- Shared General Formulas
> Some different homologous series share the same general formula:
> - **Alkenes and cycloalkanes:** ||$\ce{C_{n}H_{2n}}$|| 
> - **Alkynes and cycloalkenes:** ||$\ce{C_{n}H_{2n-2}}$|| 
> - **Aldehydes and ketones:** ||$\ce{C_{n}H_{2n}O}$|| 
> - **Carboxylic acids and esters:** ||$\ce{C_{n}H_{2n}O_{2}}$|| 

> [!table]- Summary of General Formulas
> | Homologous series | General Formula | Example | Structural Formula |
> | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
> | Alkanes | $\ce{C_{n}H_{2n+2}}$ | $\ce{C6H14}$ | $\ce{CH3(CH2)4CH3}$ |

> | Alkynes | $\ce{C_{n}H_{2n-2}}$ | $\ce{C3H4}$ | $\ce{CH3C\equiv CH}$ |
> | Haloalkanes | $\ce{C_{n}H_{2n+1}X}$ | $\ce{C3H7Br}$ | $\ce{CH3CH2CH2Br}$ |
> | Alcohols | $\ce{C_{n}H_{2n+2}O}$ | $\ce{C3H7OH}$ | $\ce{CH3CH2CH2OH}$ |
> | Aldehydes | $\ce{C_{n}H_{2n}O}$ | $\ce{C4H8O}$ | $\ce{CH3CH2CH2CH=O}$ |

# 6.2 IUPAC Nomenclature

> [!definition]- The IUPAC nomenclature system
> is a set of rules used by organic chemists to name organic compounds.
> ![[CHAPTER 6 2026-02-11 00.42.38.excalidraw]]
> > [!blank]- $\color{orange}i$
> > Numbers of location of substituent
> 
> > [!blank]- $\color{orange}ii$
> > Family name
> 
> > [!blank]- $\color{orange}iii$
> > Base (root) name
> 
> > [!blank]- $\color{orange}v$
> > Longest chain
> 
> > [!blank]- $\color{orange}v$
> > Substituents (branches) on longest chain.

## Organic Nomenclature
> [!liststart]- Organic compounds are divided into several different families or groups
> > [!note]- The name varies slightly depending on which group it is in
> 
> In general, each name has the following components:
> > [!continue]- Prefix
> > identifies each of the ||substituents|| and gives the location of the ||substituents|| and ||functional group||
> 
> > [!continue]- Parent Name
> > the number of ||Carbon|| atoms in the longest continuous chain
> 
> > [!continue]- Suffix
> > indicates the ||family group|| (it identifies the functional group(s) present in the molecules)

## General rules for naming organic compounds

> [!procedure]- Determine the Parent name
> > [!continue]- ||Count|| each continuous chain to
> > find the ||longest|| (some chains may turn corners)
> 
> > [!continue]- ||Name|| the Parent, according to
> > the ||longest|| continuous chain (ref. [[#List of Parent Names]])
> 
> > [!continue]- If you have two chains equal in length
> > choose the one with the ||larger number of branches||
> 
> > [!continue]- If the parent chain is cyclical
> > add the word '||cyclo||' preceding the Parent name
> 
> > [!continue]- When naming a molecule, the parent chain must include
> > the ||functional group(s)||
> > > [!info]- Exception:
> > > alkanes

> [!procedure]- Assign each carbon atom in the parent chain a number
> > [!continue]- Begin counting the parent chain at
> > the end nearer the ||first|| branch
> 
> > [!continue]- If the first branch occurs at 
> > the same ||carbon|| number when counting from either end of the parent chain
> > > [!continue]- then
> > > begin at the end that gives the ||lowest|| number for the ||second|| branch.
> 
> > [!continue]- When naming a molecule, number the chain so that the ||lowest|| number is given to the ||first carbon|| of the functional group
> > > [!info]- exception:
> > > Alkane

> [!procedure]- Name the prefix
> > [!continue]- ||identify|| each substituent present.
> > Once identified, the substituent name can be assigned (ref.[[#List of Substituent Names]])
> 
> > [!continue]- ||Count|| the number of each ||identical substituents||
> > Give the substituent name a prefix according to ||the number of identical substituent present|| (ref. [[#List of Prefixes]])
> 
> > [!continue]- Assign a number to
> > each ||substituent||, matching the number of the ||carbon atom|| where it attaches to the parent chain.
> > > [!continue]- Preceding the name of each substituent and prefix to ||substituent||, give the number for the ||substituent|| followed by a ||hyphen (~)||
> 
> > [!continue]- If two substituents are present on the same carbon atom
> > assign them both the same number.
> 
> > [!continue]- Write the Prefix as a single word
> >  using ||hyphens (~)|| to separate the different substituent names and ||commas (,)|| to separate the numbers
> 
> > [!continue]- List the substituent name alphabetically, according to
> > the root name

> [!procedure]- Name the suffix
> > [!continue]- Determined by the family name (ref. [[#List of Suffixes]])
> 
> > [!continue]- If more than one different functional groups are present
> > list their suffixes in ||alphabetical order||, with ||hyphenated numbers|| used to indicate the ||carbon number|| of the parent chain that they are bonded to.
> 
> > [!continue]- If more than one identical functional group is present
> > give the suffix a prefix according to the number present. (ref. [[#List of Prefixes]])

### Step 1: Find the Parent Hydrocarbon Chain

> [!procedure]- Rule A: Longest Continuous Chain
> Find the ||longest continuous chain|| of carbon atoms as the parent compound.
> > [!note]- Shape does not matter
> > It doesn't matter whether the carbon skeleton is drawn in an extended ||straight-chain form|| or in one with many ||bends and turns||.
> > > [!example]- Examples
> > > - **3-methylhexane:** Named as a substituted ||hexane||.
> > > > [!picture]- 
> > > > ![[CHAPTER 6 2026-02-10 12.33.20.excalidraw]]
> > > 
> > > - **4-ethyl-3-methylheptane:** Named as a substituted ||heptane||.
> > > ![[CHAPTER 6 2026-02-10 12.37.59.excalidraw]]

> [!procedure]- Rule B: Tie-Breaker for Equal Lengths
> If two different chains of ||equal length|| are present, choose the one with the ||larger number of branch points|| as the parent.
> > [!example]- Comparison
> > > [!picture]- Correct
> > > Named as a ||hexane|| with ||two substituents||.
> > > ![[CHAPTER 6 2026-02-10 12.40.54.excalidraw]]

> > > Named as a ||hexane|| with ||one substituent||.
> > > ![[CHAPTER 6 2026-02-10 12.45.05.excalidraw]]

### Step 2: Number the Atoms in the Main Chain

> [!procedure]- Rule A: Nearer the First Branch
> Beginning at the end ||nearer the first branch point||, number each carbon atom in the parent chain.
> > [!example]- Correct Numbering
> > The first branch occurs at ||C3|| in the proper system of numbering (not C4).
> > ![[Pasted image 20260210124634.png]]

> [!procedure]- Rule B: Tie-Breaker for Equidistant Branching
> If there is branching an ||equal distance|| away from both ends of the parent chain, begin numbering at the end nearer the ||second branch point||.
> > [!example]- Comparison
> > > [!picture]- Correct
> > > Numbering starts from the right to give lower numbers to substituents.
> > > ![[Pasted image 20260210124705.png]]
> > 
> > > [!picture]- Incorrect
> > > Numbering starts from the left.
> > > ![[Pasted image 20260210124712.png]]

### Step 3: Identify and Number the Substituents

> [!procedure]- Rule A: Assign Locants
> Assign a number, called a ||locant||, to each substituent to locate its point of attachment to the parent chain.
> > [!example]- 4-ethyl-3,7-dimethylnonane
> > - Parent: ||Nonane|| (9 carbons)
> > - Substituents:
> >     - on C3: $\ce{CH_{2}CH_{3}}$ (||3-ethyl||)
> >     - on C4: $\ce{CH_{3}}$ (||4-methyl||)
> >     - on C7: $\ce{CH_{3}}$ (||7-methyl||)
> > ![[Pasted image 20260210124734.png]]

> [!procedure]- Rule B: Multiple Substituents on Same Carbon
> If there are two substituents on the ||same carbon||, give both the ||same number||.
> > [!note]- Number Count
> > There must be as many numbers in the name as there are ||substituents||.
> > > [!example]- 4-ethyl-2,4-dimethylhexane
> > > - Parent: ||Hexane||
> > > - Substituents:
> > >     - on C2: $\ce{CH_{3}}$ (||2-methyl||)
> > >     - on C4: $\ce{CH_{3}}$ (||4-methyl||)
> > >     - on C4: $\ce{CH_{2}CH_{3}}$ (||4-ethyl||)
> > > ![[Pasted image 20260210124747.png]]

## Nomenclature of Alkynes

> [!procedure]- Step 1: Find the Parent Chain
> Find the ||longest, continuous C chain|| that contains the ||triple bond|| and use it to determine the base name.
> > [!example]- 1-Octyne
> > Longest carbon chain is ||8||.
> > ![[Pasted image 20260211084844.png]]

> [!procedure]- Step 2: Number the Chain
> Determine the end ||closest to the triple bond||.
> > [!info]- Tie-Breaker
> > If the triple bond is ||equidistant|| from both ends, number from the end closest to the ||substituents||.

> [!procedure]- Step 3: Identify Substituents
> Assign numbers to each substituent based on the number of the main chain C it is attached to.
> > [!example]- Identification
> > - **C-4:** ||Ethyl||
> > - **C-6:** ||Isopropyl||
> > ![[Pasted image 20260211085015.png]]

> > [!liststart]- Order
> > 1. Substituent number of first ||alphabetical|| substituent.
> > 2. Substituent name (use prefixes for multiple identical).
> > 3. Repeat for other substituents.
> > 4. Number of first C in ||triple bond||.
> > 5. Name of main chain.
> > 
> > > [!example]- 4-ethyl-6-isopropyloctyne
> > > $\textcolor[RGB]{150,150,250}{\text{4-ethyl}}$-$\textcolor[RGB]{0,180,0}{\text{6-isopropyl}}$octyne

> > > Common name: ||ethyl methyl ether||

> >
> > > [!continue]- Positive Test
> > > The yellow color of bromine is ||discharged (disappears)|| for alkenes and alkynes. The solution is decolorised.

> > > [!example]- Reaction with Inert Solvent
> > > Alkene + $\ce{Br2}$/$\ce{CH2Cl2}$ (yellow-brown colour) $\rightarrow$ ||colourless||

> >

> > > ![[Pasted image 20260214001231.png]]

> [!definition]- Baeyer's Test ($\ce{KMnO4}$)

> > > 
> >
> > > [!example]- Chemical Equation

> [!definition]- Tollen's Test
> A specific test to distinguish ||aldehydes|| from ketones. Aldehydes are easily oxidised using mild oxidising agents, whereas ||ketones|| would not oxidise even using strong oxidising agents.

> > > [!continue]- Reagent

> > > [!continue]- Positive Test

> > >

> > > [!continue]- Positive Test

> >
> > > [!continue]- Reaction Mechanism & Equation
> > > The hydrogen attached to the carbon next to the carbonyl carbon are substituted by halogens.
> >

## Polymerization Reactions (Formation of Polymers)

> [!liststart]- Methods of Preparing Polymers
> There are a number of different methods of preparing polymers from suitable monomers:
> > [!continue]- i. Chain polymerization (or Addition polymerization) 
> 
> > [!continue]- ii. Stepwise polymerization (or Condensation polymerization) 
> 
> > [!continue]- iii. Insertion polymerization 

### i. Chain Polymerization (Addition Polymerization)

> [!definition]- Chain-Growth Polymers
> Also known as ||addition polymers||, they are made by ||chain reactions||. 
> > [!liststart]- Characteristics
> > > [!continue]- Multi-step Process
> > Occurs in a multi-step process involving the ||initiation||, ||propagation||, and ||termination|| steps.
> > 
> > > [!continue]- Monomer Requirements
> > > The monomers involved must have a ||$\ce{C=C}$|| (carbon double bond) .
> > The double bond breaks to allow carbon to form new bonds with other compounds.
> > 
> > > [!continue]- Polymer Composition
> > The polymer formed has the ||same atoms|| as the monomers.
> > 
> > > [!example]- Examples
> > ||Polystyrene|| and ||polyvinyl chloride (PVC)||.
> > ![[Pasted image 20260214010557.png]]

> [!structure]- Mechanism Steps
> > [!continue]- Step 1: Initiation
> > An ||initiator|| is added to form a ||free radical|| which reacts with a monomer to start a growing chain. 
> > > [!picture]- Reaction
> > > $\ce{R-R \rightarrow 2R\bullet}$ 
> > > $\ce{R\bullet + CH2=CH2 \rightarrow R-CH2CH2\bullet}$ 
> > > ![[Pasted image 20260214010633.png]]
> 
> > [!continue]- Step 2: Propagation
> > The growing chain reacts with ||more monomers|| resulting in an ||increase|| in chain length. 
> > > [!picture]- Reaction
> > > $\ce{R-CH2CH2\bullet + CH2=CH2 \rightarrow R-CH2CH2CH2CH2\bullet}$ 
> > > ![[Pasted image 20260214010752.png]]
> > > 
> > > $\ce{R-CH2CH2CH2CH2\bullet + CH2=CH2 \rightarrow R-CH2CH2CH2CH2CH2CH2\bullet}$ 
> > > ![[Pasted image 20260214010732.png]]
> 
> > [!continue]- Step 3: Termination
> > Two growing chains react resulting in an ||'unreactive'|| or ||'dead'|| polymer. 
> > > [!picture]- Reaction
> > > $\ce{2 R-(CH2CH2)_{n}CH2CH2\bullet \rightarrow R-(CH2CH2)_{n}CH2CH2-CH2CH2(CH2CH2)_{n}R}$ 
> > > ![[Pasted image 20260214010901.png]]

### ii. Stepwise Polymerization (Condensation Polymerization)

> [!definition]- Step-Growth Polymers
> Also called ||condensation polymers||, this occurs when monomers link together by ||eliminating a small stable molecule|| (such as water or $\ce{HCl}$) . 
> > [!liststart]- Characteristics
> > > [!continue]- Monomer Requirements
> > > The monomers involved must have ||two reactive end groups||.
> > 
> > > [!example]- Examples
> > > ||Nylon|| (joined together by ||amide links||) and ||Polyester|| (joined together by ||ester links||) .

## Crystalline and Amorphous Polymer

> [!structure]- Physical States of Polymers
> Polymers can present in these three physical states:
> > [!continue]- i. Amorphous
> > ||Lack|| ordered molecular structure.
> > When heated, they soften over a wide temperature range known as the ||glass transition temperature||, ($T_g$).
> > > [!example]- 
> > > Polystyrene and poly(methyl methacrylate).
> 
> > [!continue]- ii. Crystalline
> > Have ||ordered|| molecular structure. 
> > They have ||melting points||, ($T_m$) (the temperature at which the ordered regions break).
> > > [!example]-
> > > Polyethylene, PET polyester.
> 
> > [!continue]- iii. Liquid Crystals
> > Polymers that show ||intermediate phases|| (a subject actively researched in industry and academia) .

## Major Polymer Types

> [!structure]- Classification based on Forces and Shapes
> > [!continue]- Fibers
> > Have ||strong forces|| between the polymer chains and the chains are lined up by ||stretching||. 
> 
> > [!continue]- Plastics
> > Have ||moderate forces|| between the polymer chains and are classified as follows:
> > > [!continue]- Thermoplastics
> > > ||Soften|| when heated and ||can be reshaped||.
> > > E.g., chain polymers, polyesters, and nylons (typically linear or branched).
> > 
> > > [!continue]- Thermosets
> > > ||Do not soften|| when heated but become harder as they become more ||crosslinked|| (network structure) .
> 
> > [!continue]- Elastomers
> > Have ||weak forces|| between the polymer chains and their shapes are maintained by ||crosslinking||.
