## Acid-Base Chemistry

> [!definition]- Arrhenius Acid
> A substance that generates **hydrogen ions ($H^+$)** in aqueous solutions.

> [!definition]- Arrhenius Base
> A substance that generates **hydroxide ions ($OH^-$)** in aqueous solutions.

> [!definition]- Brønsted-Lowry Acid
> A substance that **donates** a **proton ($H^+$)** to another substance.

> [!definition]- Brønsted-Lowry Base
> A substance that **accepts** a **proton ($H^+$)** from another substance.

> [!definition]- Amphoteric Substance
> A substance that can act as either an **acid** or a **base**.

> [!definition]- Lewis Acid
> A substance that **accepts** an **electron pair** to form a covalent bond.

> [!definition]- Lewis Base
> A substance that **donates** an **electron pair** to form a covalent bond.

> [!definition]- Inductive Effect
> The shift in electron density from one atom to another through the sigma ($\sigma$) bond, often represented by an arrow pointing toward the more electronegative atom.

> [!definition]- Acid Dissociation Constant ($K_a$)
> A quantitative measure of the strength of a weak acid in solution.

> [!definition]- Base Dissociation Constant ($K_b$)
> A quantitative measure of the strength of a weak base in solution.

## Salts and Titrations

> [!definition]- Salt
> An ionic compound formed from the neutralization reaction of an **acid** and a **base**.

> [!definition]- Salt Hydrolysis
> The reaction of an anion or cation of a salt with **water**, which can affect the pH of the solution.

> [!definition]- Buffer Solution
> A solution that resists significant changes in **pH** when small amounts of strong acid or strong base are added to it.

> [!definition]- Titration
> A technique where a solution of **known concentration** (titrant) is added to a solution of **unknown concentration** (analyte) until the reaction is complete.

> [!definition]- Equivalence Point
> The point in a titration where the number of moles of added titrant is stoichiometrically **equal** to the moles of analyte in the original solution.

> [!definition]- End Point
> The point in a titration where the **indicator** changes color, signaling that the titration should stop.

> [!definition]- Indicator Mechanism
> An indicator is typically a weak acid ($HIn$) that has a different color than its conjugate base ($In^-$). The color change occurs as the pH of the system shifts the equilibrium.

> [!definition]- Titration Curve
> A graph plotting the **pH** of the solution (y-axis) against the **volume** of titrant added (x-axis).

## Electrochemistry

> [!definition]- Electrochemistry
> The study of the relationship between **chemical energy** and **electrical energy**, specifically involving **redox reactions**.

> [!definition]- Redox Reaction
> Chemical reactions that involve the **transfer of electrons** from one species to another.

> [!definition]- Oxidizing Agent
> A species that causes the **oxidation** of another species by accepting electrons itself (it is reduced).

> [!definition]- Reducing Agent
> A species that causes the **reduction** of another species by donating electrons (it is oxidized).

> [!definition]- Oxidation Number
> Also known as **Oxidation State**, it represents the total number of electrons that an atom either gains or loses in order to form a chemical bond with another atom.

> [!definition]- Voltaic Cell (Galvanic Cell)
> An electrochemical cell that produces **electrical** current derived from **spontaneous** redox reactions.

> [!definition]- Inert Electrode
> An electrode that conducts electrons into or out of a cell but does **not** participate in the chemical reaction itself (e.g., Platinum or Graphite).

> [!definition]- Potential Difference
> The difference in **potential energy** per unit charge between two points in a circuit.

> [!definition]- Electromotive Force (EMF)
> The maximum potential difference between two electrodes of a voltaic cell; the "force" pushing electrons through the circuit.

> [!definition]- Cell Potential ($E_{cell}$)
> The difference in potential energy between the anode and the cathode in a voltaic cell.

> [!definition]- Standard Reduction Potential ($E^\circ_{red}$)
> The potential, measured under standard conditions (25°C, 1 M, 1 atm), for a reduction half-cell.

> [!definition]- Standard Oxidation Potential ($E^\circ_{oxi}$)
> The potential, measured under standard conditions, for an oxidation half-cell. Note: $E^\circ_{oxi} = -E^\circ_{red}$.

> [!definition]- Corrosion
> A natural chemical process that causes the deterioration of metals via electrochemical reaction with the environment (e.g., rusting).

> [!definition]- Fuel Cell
> A galvanic cell that requires a continuous external supply of reactants (gases like Hydrogen and Oxygen) to produce electricity, using catalysts to facilitate the reaction.
