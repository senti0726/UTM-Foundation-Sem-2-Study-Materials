# 3.1 Dynamic Equilibrium in Chemical Systems

## Reversible and Irreversible Reactions

> [!compare]- Irreversible vs Reversible
> > [!continue]- Irreversible Reaction
> > A reaction that takes place in $\color{orange}i$ direction almost exclusively.
> > * Reaction goes to ||completion||.
> > * Example: $\ce{2Mg(s) + O2(g) -> 2MgO(s)}$
> > > [!blank]- $\color{orange}i$
> > > one
>
> > [!continue]- Reversible Reaction
> > A reaction that takes place in $\color{orange}ii$ directions at comparable rates.
> > * Example: $\ce{CaCO3(s) <=> CaO(s) + CO2(g)}$
> > * Example: $\ce{N2(g) + 3H2(g) <=> 2NH3(g)}$
> > > [!blank]- $\color{orange}ii$
> > > both (forward and reverse)

## Dynamic Equilibrium

> [!definition]- Dynamic Equilibrium
> A state reached when the rate of the $\color{orange}i$ reaction is equal to the rate of the $\color{orange}ii$ reaction.
> * The reaction may look "finished", but both reactions continue to occur at the ||same rate||.
> 
> > [!blank]- $\color{orange}i$
> > forward
>
> > [!blank]- $\color{orange}ii$
> > reverse

# 3.1 Dynamic equilibrium in chemical systems

> [!info]- Context
> Chemical reactions carried out in a $\color{orange}i$ generally achieve an equilibrium state.
> > [!blank]- $\color{orange}i$
> > closed vessel

## The Equilibrium State

> [!definition]- Dynamic Equilibrium
> A state where the reaction may look "finished", but the $\color{orange}i$ and $\color{orange}ii$ reactions actually continue to happen at the $\color{orange}iii$.
> > [!blank]- $\color{orange}i$
> > forward
>
> > [!blank]- $\color{orange}ii$
> > reverse
>
> > [!blank]- $\color{orange}iii$
> > same rate

> [!compare]- Forward vs Reverse Reaction
> > [!continue]- Forward Reaction
> > Reaction proceeds from $\color{orange}i$ to $\color{orange}ii$.
> > > [!blank]- $\color{orange}i$
> > > left
> >
> > > [!blank]- $\color{orange}ii$
> > > right
>
> > [!continue]- Reverse Reaction
> > Reaction proceeds from $\color{orange}iii$ to $\color{orange}iv$.
> > > [!blank]- $\color{orange}iii$
> > > right
> >
> > > [!blank]- $\color{orange}iv$
> > > left

## The Equilibrium Constant ($K$)

> [!formula]- General Equilibrium Expression
> For the reaction: $\ce{aA + bB <=> cC + dD}$
> $K = \frac{[\text{C}]^c [\text{D}]^d}{[\text{A}]^a [\text{B}]^b}$
> > [!unit]- $K$
> > Equilibrium constant (dependent on ||temperature||).
>
> > [!unit]- $[\text{X}]$
> > Molar concentration of species X (products over reactants).
>
> > [!unit]- $a,b,c,d$
> > Stoichiometric coefficients from the balanced equation.

> [!liststart]- Types of Equilibrium Constants
> > [!continue]- Concentration ($K_c$)
> > Expressed in terms of molar concentrations.
> > $K_c = \frac{[\ce{CS2}][\ce{H2}]^4}{[\ce{CH4}][\ce{H2S}]^2}$
>
> > [!continue]- Pressure ($K_p$)
> > Expressed in terms of partial pressures (for gases).
> > $K_p = \frac{(P_{\ce{CS2}})(P_{\ce{H2}})^4}{(P_{\ce{CH4}})(P_{\ce{H2S}})^2}$

> [!sq]- Sample Calculation: Finding $K_p$
> Reaction: $\ce{H2(g) + I2(g) <=> 2HI(g)}$ at $425^\circ \text{C}$.
> **Initial:** $P_{\ce{H2}} = 5.73\,\text{atm}$, $P_{\ce{I2}} = 5.73\,\text{atm}$, $P_{\ce{HI}} = 0$.
> **Equilibrium:** $P_{\ce{HI}} = 9.00\,\text{atm}$.
> Calculate $K_p$.
> > [!continue]- Step 1: ICE Table (Pressure)
> >
> > | Species | $\ce{H2}$ | $\ce{I2}$ | $\ce{2HI}$ |
> > | :--- | :---: | :---: | :---: |
> > | **I**nitial | $5.73$ | $5.73$ | $0$ |
> > | **C**hange | $-x$ | $-x$ | $+2x$ |
> > | **E**quilibrium | $5.73-x$ | $5.73-x$ | $2x$ |
>
> > [!continue]- Step 2: Solve for $x$
> > Given Equilibrium $P_{\ce{HI}} = 9.00\,\text{atm}$.
> > $2x = 9.00 \implies x = 4.50\,\text{atm}$.
>
> > [!continue]- Step 3: Find Equilibrium Pressures
> > $P_{\ce{H2}} = 5.73 - 4.50 = 1.23\,\text{atm}$.
> > $P_{\ce{I2}} = 5.73 - 4.50 = 1.23\,\text{atm}$.
>
> > [!continue]- Step 4: Calculate $K_p$
> > $K_p = \frac{(P_{\ce{HI}})^2}{(P_{\ce{H2}})(P_{\ce{I2}})}$
> > $K_p = \frac{(9.00)^2}{(1.23)(1.23)} = \frac{81.0}{1.5129}$
> > $\enclose{box}{K_p = 53.5}$

## Homogeneous vs Heterogeneous Equilibria

> [!compare]- Types of Equilibria
> > [!continue]- Homogeneous Equilibrium
> > Involves reactants and products in the ||same|| state of matter.
>
> > [!continue]- Heterogeneous Equilibrium
> > Involves reactants and products in ||different|| states of matter.
> > * **Rule:** Pure solids ($s$) and pure liquids ($l$) have constant concentrations and **do not appear** in the equilibrium expression.

> [!sq]- Examples: Writing Expressions
> > [!continue]- Heterogeneous 1
> > $\ce{H2(g) + FeO(s) <=> H2O(g) + Fe(s)}$
> > > [!continue]- $K_c =$
> > > $\frac{[\ce{H2O}]}{[\ce{H2}]}$ (Solids omitted).
>
> > [!continue]- Heterogeneous 2
> > $\ce{Ag+(aq) + Fe^{2+}(aq) <=> Fe^{3+}(aq) + Ag(s)}$
> > > [!continue]- $K_c =$
> > > $\frac{[\ce{Fe^{3+}}]}{[\ce{Ag+}][\ce{Fe^{2+}}]}$ (Solid Ag omitted).

# 3.2 Equilibrium Position and Stoichiometry

## Magnitude of Equilibrium Constant ($K$)

> [!liststart]- Relation to Equilibrium Position
> The value of $K$ indicates the extent of the reaction (how far reactants are converted to products).
> > [!continue]- $K \gg 1$ (e.g., $>10^2$)
> > Equilibrium position lies to the $\color{orange}i$.
> > * The ratio of products to reactants is ||high||.
> > * Reaction "goes to completion" (mostly products).
> > * Example: $\ce{2NO(g) <=> N2(g) + O2(g)}$ ($K_c = 1.0 \times 10^{30}$).
> > > [!blank]- $\color{orange}i$
> > > right
>
> > [!continue]- $K \ll 1$ (e.g., $<10^{-2}$)
> > Equilibrium position lies to the $\color{orange}ii$.
> > * The ratio of products to reactants is ||low||.
> > * Reaction hardly proceeds (mostly reactants).
> > * Example: $\ce{COCl2(g) <=> CO(g) + Cl2(g)}$ ($K_c = 2.19 \times 10^{-10}$).
> > > [!blank]- $\color{orange}ii$
> > > left
>
> > [!continue]- $K \approx 1$ ($10^{-2} < K < 10^2$)
> > Equilibrium position is intermediate.
> > * The conversion of reactants to products is ||almost equal|| (comparable amounts of both).

## Manipulating Equilibrium Constants

> [!liststart]- Rules for $K$
> > [!continue]- Reversing the Reaction
> > If the reaction equation is reversed, the new equilibrium constant ($K'$) is the $\color{orange}i$ of the original $K$.
> > $K' = \frac{1}{K} = K^{-1}$
> > > [!blank]- $\color{orange}i$
> > > reciprocal (inverse)
>
> > [!continue]- Multiplying Coefficients
> > If the stoichiometric coefficients of the equation are multiplied by a factor $n$, the new equilibrium constant is the original $K$ raised to the power of $\color{orange}ii$.
> > $K' = K^n$
> > > [!blank]- $\color{orange}ii$
> > > $n$

> [!sq]- Sample Problem 1: Calculating $K$ and Direction
> One-litre vessel contains a mixture of $1.75\,mol$ of $\ce{HI}$, $1.125mol$ of $\ce{H_{2}}$ and $0.125mol$ of $\ce{l_{2}}$, in  equilibrium at $20^{\circ}C$
> > [!continue]- Write the equilibrium constant expression and calculate the forward and reverse reaction
> > 
> > **Forward:** $K_c = \frac{[\ce{H2}][\ce{I2}]}{[\ce{HI}]^2} = \frac{(1.125)(0.125)}{(1.75)^2}$
> > $\enclose{box}{K_c = 0.046}$
> >
> > **Reverse:** $\ce{H2 + I2 <=> 2HI}$
> > $K'_c = \frac{1}{K_c} = \frac{1}{0.046}$
> > $\enclose{box}{K'_c = 21.74}$
>
> > [!continue]- (ii) For the forward reaction, predict the equilibrium position and formation of products
> > Since $K_c = 0.046$ (which is $< 1$), the equilibrium position lies to the ||left||.
> > Formation of products is ||low||.

> [!sq]- Sample Problem 2: Manipulating $K$
> The following partial pressures of a mixture of gases are measured at equilibrium conditions: $P_{\ce{SO2}} = 0.05\,\text{atm}$, $P_{\ce{O2}} = 0.03\,\text{atm}$, $P_{\ce{SO3}} = 1.00\,\text{atm}$.
> > [!continue]- (a) Calculate $K_p$ for $\ce{2SO2 + O2 <=> 2SO3}$
> > $K_p = \frac{(P_{\ce{SO3}})^2}{(P_{\ce{SO2}})^2 (P_{\ce{O2}})} = \frac{(1.00)^2}{(0.05)^2 (0.03)}$
> > $K_p = \frac{1}{0.0025 \times 0.03} = \frac{1}{0.000075}$
> > $\enclose{box}{K_p = 1.33 \times 10^4\,\text{atm}^{-1}}$
>
> > [!continue]- (b) Calculate $K_p$ for $\ce{SO2(g) + 1/2 O2(g) <=> SO3(g)}$
> > The equation in (b) is the equation in (a) multiplied by $n = \frac{1}{2}$.
> > $K'_p = (K_p)^{1/2} = \sqrt{1.33 \times 10^4}$
> > $\enclose{box}{K'_p = 1.15 \times 10^2\,\text{atm}^{-1/2}}$

## Relationship Between $K_c$ and $K_p$

> [!formula]- The Relation Formula
> For gases, partial pressure is proportional to concentration ($P = MRT$).
> $K_p = K_c (RT)^{\Delta n}$
> > [!unit]- $K_p$
> > Equilibrium constant using partial pressures
>
> > [!unit]- $K_c$
> > Equilibrium constant using molar concentrations
>
> > [!unit]- $R$
> > Gas constant ($0.0821\,\text{L atm mol}^{-1} \text{K}^{-1}$)
>
> > [!unit]- $T$
> > Temperature in Kelvin ($\text{K}$)
>
> > [!unit]- $\Delta n$
> > (Moles of gaseous products) $-$ (Moles of gaseous reactants)
> > $\Delta n = n_{\text{gas(prod)}} - n_{\text{gas(react)}}$

> [!mnemonic]- Kp vs Kc Equation
> $\textcolor[RGB]{255,0,255}{\text{P}}$olitically $\textcolor[RGB]{255,0,255}{\text{C}}$orrect $\textcolor[RGB]{255,0,255}{\text{R}}$e$\textcolor[RGB]{255,0,255}{\text{T}}$ai$\textcolor[RGB]{255,0,255}{\text{n}}$ers
> > [!mnemonic-points]- $\mathbf{\textcolor[RGB]{255,255,0}{\text{P}}}$olitically
> > **$K_{\textcolor[RGB]{255,0,255}{\textbf{p}}}$**
> > The equilibrium constant for partial pressure.
> > *Stands on the left side of the equation.*
>
> > [!mnemonic-points]- $\mathbf{\textcolor[RGB]{255,255,0}{\text{C}}}$orrect
> > **= $K_{\textcolor[RGB]{255,0,255}{\textbf{c}}}$**
> > The equilibrium constant for concentration.
>
> > [!mnemonic-points]- $\mathbf{\textcolor[RGB]{255,255,0}{\text{R}}}$e$\mathbf{\textcolor[RGB]{255,255,0}{\text{T}}}$ai$\mathbf{\textcolor[RGB]{255,255,0}{\text{n}}}$ers
> > **$({\textcolor[RGB]{255,0,255}{\textbf{RT}}})^{\Delta {\textcolor[RGB]{255,0,255}{\textbf{n}}}}$**
> > The conversion factor.
> > * $\Delta n$ = (moles gaseous product) - (moles gaseous reactant)
> > * Formula: $K_p = K_c(RT)^{\Delta n}$

> [!procedure]- Calculation Example: $K_p$ to $K_c$
> **Reaction:** $\ce{2Cl2(g) + 2H2O(g) <=> 4HCl(g) + O2(g)}$ at $480^\circ \text{C}$.
> **Given:** $K_p = 0.0752$. Calculate $K_c$.
> > [!continue]- Step 1: Calculate $\Delta n$
> > Products: $4 (\ce{HCl}) + 1 (\ce{O2}) = 5$ moles gas.
> > Reactants: $2 (\ce{Cl2}) + 2 (\ce{H2O}) = 4$ moles gas.
> > $\Delta n = 5 - 4 = +1$.
>
> > [!continue]- Step 2: Convert Temperature
> > $T = 480 + 273 = 753\,\text{K}$.
>
> > [!continue]- Step 3: Solve for $K_c$
> > $K_p = K_c (RT)^{\Delta n} \implies K_c = \frac{K_p}{(RT)^{\Delta n}}$
> > $K_c = \frac{0.0752}{[(0.0821)(753)]^1} = \frac{0.0752}{61.82}$
> > $\enclose{box}{K_c = 1.22 \times 10^{-3}}$

# 3.3 Predicting the Direction of a Reaction

> [!info]- Context
> The direction in which a reaction will initially move to achieve equilibrium can be predicted by comparing the $\color{orange}i$ and $\color{orange}ii$ values.
> > [!blank]- $\color{orange}i$
> > Reaction Quotient ($Q_c$)
>
> > [!blank]- $\color{orange}ii$
> > Equilibrium Constant ($K_c$)

## The Reaction Quotient ($Q_c$)

> [!definition]- Reaction Quotient ($Q$)
> The value obtained by substituting **initial concentrations** (or partial pressures) into the equilibrium constant expression.
> * **Purpose:** To determine if a system is at equilibrium, and if not, which direction it will shift.

> [!formula]- Formula
> $Q_c = \frac{[\text{C}]_0^c [\text{D}]_0^d}{[\text{A}]_0^a [\text{B}]_0^b}$
> > [!unit]- $[\text{X}]_0$
> > Initial concentration of species X (before equilibrium)
>
> > [!tip]- Difference from $K$
> > * **Calculate $Q$:** Use ||initial|| concentrations.
> > * **Calculate $K$:** Use ||equilibrium|| concentrations.

## Predicting Shifts

> [!compare]- Comparing $Q$ and $K$
> ![[Pasted image 20260205230214.png]]
> > [!continue]- $Q_c < K_c$ (Too much Reactant)
> > The ratio of products to reactants is too small.
> > * **Shift:** Reaction proceeds to the ||right|| (forward).
> > * **Result:** Reactants decrease, Products increase.
>
> > [!continue]- $Q_c = K_c$ (At Equilibrium)
> > The system is already at equilibrium.
> > * **Shift:** ||No net change||.
>
> > [!continue]- $Q_c > K_c$ (Too much Product)
> > The ratio of products to reactants is too large.
> > * **Shift:** Reaction proceeds to the ||left|| (reverse).
> > * **Result:** Products decrease, Reactants increase.

> [!mnemonic]- Predicting Shifts (Q vs K)
> $\textcolor[RGB]{255,0,255}{\text{S}}$mall $\textcolor[RGB]{255,0,255}{\text{Q}}$ueens $\textcolor[RGB]{255,0,255}{\text{R}}$un $\textcolor[RGB]{255,0,255}{\text{R}}$ight
> > [!mnemonic-points]- $\mathbf{\textcolor[RGB]{255,255,0}{\text{S}}}$mall $\mathbf{\textcolor[RGB]{255,255,0}{\text{Q}}}$ueens
> > **If $Q_c$ is ${\textcolor[RGB]{255,0,255}{\textbf{S}}}$maller ($Q_c < K_c$)**
> > The ratio of products to reactants is too low compared to equilibrium.
>
> > [!mnemonic-points]- $\mathbf{\textcolor[RGB]{255,255,0}{\text{R}}}$un $\mathbf{\textcolor[RGB]{255,255,0}{\text{R}}}$ight
> > **Shift ${\textcolor[RGB]{255,0,255}{\textbf{R}}}$ight (Forward)**
> > The reaction proceeds forward to form more products until $Q_c = K_c$.
>
> > [!mnemonic-points]- (The Inverse)
> > **If $Q_c$ is Big ($Q_c > K_c$)**
> > The reaction shifts **Left (Reverse)** to reduce products.

> [!sq]- Sample Problem 3: Predicting Direction
> Consider the reaction: $\ce{COCl2(g) <=> CO(g) + Cl2(g)}$ with $K_c = 2.19 \times 10^{-10}$.
> The initial concentrations are:
> $[\ce{COCl2}]_0 = 3.50 \times 10^{-3}\,\text{M}$
> $[\ce{CO}]_0 = 1.10 \times 10^{-5}\,\text{M}$
> $[\ce{Cl2}]_0 = 3.25 \times 10^{-6}\,\text{M}$
> Is the reaction at equilibrium? If not, to which direction will it shift?
> > [!continue]- Step 1: Calculate $Q_c$
> > $Q_c = \frac{[\ce{CO}]_0 [\ce{Cl2}]_0}{[\ce{COCl2}]_0}$
> > $Q_c = \frac{(1.10 \times 10^{-5})(3.25 \times 10^{-6})}{3.50 \times 10^{-3}}$
> > $Q_c = \frac{3.575 \times 10^{-11}}{3.50 \times 10^{-3}}$
> > $\enclose{box}{Q_c \approx 1.02 \times 10^{-8}}$
>
> > [!continue]- Step 2: Compare with $K_c$
> > Given $K_c = 2.19 \times 10^{-10}$.
> > Since $1.02 \times 10^{-8} > 2.19 \times 10^{-10}$, we have $Q_c > K_c$.
> >
> > **Conclusion:** The system is **not** at equilibrium. It has too much product, so it will shift to the ||left||.

## Calculation of Equilibrium Concentrations

> [!sq]- Exercise 1: Perfect Square Calculation
> Consider the equilibrium reaction: $\ce{Br2(g) + Cl2(g) <=> 2BrCl(g)}$ with $K_c = 7.0$ at $400\,\text{K}$.
> If $0.50\,\text{moles}$ of $\ce{Br2}$ and $0.50\,\text{moles}$ of $\ce{Cl2}$ are introduced into a one liter container, what will be the equilibrium concentration of all the species?
> > [!continue]- Step 1: Determine Direction
> > $[\ce{Br2}]_0 = 0.5\,\text{M}$, $[\ce{Cl2}]_0 = 0.5\,\text{M}$, $[\ce{BrCl}]_0 = 0$.
> > $Q_c = 0$ (since product is 0).
> > $Q_c < K_c$, so reaction shifts to the ||right||.
>
> > [!continue]- Step 2: ICE Table
> >
> > | Species | $\ce{Br2}$ | $\ce{Cl2}$ | $\ce{2BrCl}$ |
> > | :--- | :---: | :---: | :---: |
> > | **I**nitial | $0.50$ | $0.50$ | $0$ |
> > | **C**hange | $-x$ | $-x$ | $+2x$ |
> > | **E**quilibrium | $0.50-x$ | $0.50-x$ | $2x$ |
>
> > [!continue]- Step 3: Solve for $x$
> > $K_c = \frac{[\ce{BrCl}]^2}{[\ce{Br2}][\ce{Cl2}]} \implies 7.0 = \frac{(2x)^2}{(0.5-x)(0.5-x)}$
> > $7.0 = \frac{(2x)^2}{(0.5-x)^2}$
> > Take square root of both sides:
> > $\sqrt{7.0} = \frac{2x}{0.5-x} \implies 2.646 = \frac{2x}{0.5-x}$
> > $2.646(0.5 - x) = 2x$
> > $1.323 - 2.646x = 2x$
> > $1.323 = 4.646x$
> > $x = \frac{1.323}{4.646} \approx 0.285\,\text{M}$
>
> > [!continue]- Step 4: Calculate Concentrations
> > $[\ce{BrCl}] = 2x = 2(0.285) = \enclose{box}{0.570\,\text{M}}$
> > $[\ce{Br2}] = 0.5 - 0.285 = \enclose{box}{0.215\,\text{M}}$

> [!sq]- Exercise 2: Quadratic Formula Calculation
> The following equilibrium exists for $\ce{HBr}$ gas: $\ce{2HBr(g) <=> H2(g) + Br2(g)}$.
> $K_c = 6.25 \times 10^{-6}$ at $1024\,\text{K}$.
> What will be the equilibrium concentrations of all species if a container initially containing $1.00\,\text{M HBr}$ is allowed to reach equilibrium?
> > [!continue]- Step 1: ICE Table
> >
> > | Species | $\ce{2HBr}$ | $\ce{H2}$ | $\ce{Br2}$ |
> > | :--- | :---: | :---: | :---: |
> > | **I**nitial | $1.00$ | $0$ | $0$ |
> > | **C**hange | $-2x$ | $+x$ | $+x$ |
> > | **E**quilibrium | $1.00-2x$ | $x$ | $x$ |
>
> > [!continue]- Step 2: Set up Equation
> > $K_c = \frac{[\ce{H2}][\ce{Br2}]}{[\ce{HBr}]^2}$
> > $6.25 \times 10^{-6} = \frac{(x)(x)}{(1-2x)^2}$
> > Take square root:
> > $\sqrt{6.25 \times 10^{-6}} = \frac{x}{1-2x}$
> > $2.5 \times 10^{-3} = \frac{x}{1-2x}$
> >
> > **Solve for x:**
> > $2.5 \times 10^{-3} (1 - 2x) = x$
> > $0.0025 - 0.005x = x$
> > $0.0025 = 1.005x$
> > $x = \frac{0.0025}{1.005} \approx 2.49 \times 10^{-3}\,\text{M}$
>
> > [!continue]- Step 3: Calculate Concentrations
> > $[\ce{HBr}] = 1.00 - 2(2.49 \times 10^{-3}) = \enclose{box}{0.995\,\text{M}}$
> > $[\ce{H2}] = \enclose{box}{2.49 \times 10^{-3}\,\text{M}}$
> > $[\ce{Br2}] = \enclose{box}{2.49 \times 10^{-3}\,\text{M}}$

# 3.4 Factors Affecting Equilibrium and Le Chatelier's Principle

> [!definition]- Le Chatelier's Principle
> States that when a system at equilibrium is **stressed** (disturbed), the system shifts so as to $\color{orange}i$ the effect of the disturbance.
> > [!blank]- $\color{orange}i$
> > counteract (reduce)

> [!mnemonic]- Factors Affecting Equilibrium
> $\textcolor[RGB]{255,0,255}{\text{Co}}$$\textcolor[RGB]{255,255,255}{\text{st}}$ $\textcolor[RGB]{255,0,255}{\text{Pr}}$$\textcolor[RGB]{255,255,255}{\text{obably}}$ $\textcolor[RGB]{255,0,255}{\text{T}}$$\textcolor[RGB]{255,255,255}{\text{oo}}$ $\textcolor[RGB]{255,0,255}{\text{Ca}}$$\textcolor[RGB]{255,255,255}{\text{pped}}$
> **Reference Reaction:** $\ce{6CO2(g) + 6H2O(g) <=> C6H12O6(s) + 6O2(g)}$ ($\Delta H = +2816\,\text{kJ}$)
> > [!mnemonic-points]- $\mathbf{\textcolor[RGB]{255,255,0}{\text{Co}}}$st
> > **1. ${\textcolor[RGB]{255,0,255}{\textbf{Co}}}$ncentration**
> > Changes in concentration cause the system to consume added species or replace removed species.
> > > [!continue]- **Increase Reactant ($\ce{CO2}$):**
> > > System shifts to the ||right|| (to consume excess).
> >
> > > [!continue]- **Increase Product ($\ce{O2}$):**
> > > System shifts to the ||left|| (to consume excess).
> >
> > > [!continue]- **Remove Reactant ($\ce{H2O}$):**
> > > System shifts to the ||left|| (to replace it).
> >
> > > [!continue]- **Remove Solid ($\ce{C6H12O6}$):**
> > > ||No shift|| (Solids/Liquids do not affect equilibrium position).
>
> > [!mnemonic-points]- $\mathbf{\textcolor[RGB]{255,255,0}{\text{Pr}}}$obably
> > **2. ${\textcolor[RGB]{255,0,255}{\textbf{Pr}}}$essure (Volume)**
> > Affects gases only. Depends on the number of moles of gas ($\Delta n_{gas}$).
> > > [!continue]- **Increase Pressure (Reduce Volume):**
> > > Shifts to the side with ||fewer|| moles of gas.
> > > * Example: Reactants ($12\,\text{mol}$) $\to$ Products ($6\,\text{mol}$). Shift ||Right||.
> >
> > > [!continue]- **Add Inert Gas:**
> > > ||No shift|| (if partial pressures remain constant).
>
> > [!mnemonic-points]- $\mathbf{\textcolor[RGB]{255,255,0}{\text{T}}}$oo
> > **3. ${\textcolor[RGB]{255,0,255}{\textbf{T}}}$emperature**
> > The only factor that changes the value of $\color{orange}i$.
> > > [!blank]- $\color{orange}i$
> > > $K$ (Equilibrium Constant)
> >
> > > [!continue]- **Endothermic Reaction ($\Delta H > 0$):**
> > > Heat is considered a ||reactant||.
> > > * **Increase $T$:** System shifts ||right|| (to consume heat).
> > > * **Effect on $K$:** Value of $K$ ||increases||.
> >
> > > [!continue]- **Exothermic Reaction ($\Delta H < 0$):**
> > > Heat is considered a ||product||.
> > > * **Increase $T$:** System shifts ||left|| (to consume heat).
> > > * **Effect on $K$:** Value of $K$ ||decreases||.
>
> > [!mnemonic-points]- $\mathbf{\textcolor[RGB]{255,255,0}{\text{Ca}}}$pped
> > **4. ${\textcolor[RGB]{255,0,255}{\textbf{Ca}}}$talyst**
> > A substance that increases the rate without being consumed.
> > > [!continue]- **Mechanism:**
> > > Lowers the ||activation energy|| ($E_a$) for both forward and reverse reactions by the same amount.
> >
> > > [!continue]- **Effect on Equilibrium:**
> > > ||No shift|| in equilibrium position.
> > > It only increases the ||rate|| (equilibrium is reached faster).

## Summary of Shifts

> [!table]- System Response to Stress
> | Stress Imposed | System's Response | Result |
> | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **[Reactant] Increased** | Shift to Right (decrease reactant) | [Product] Increased |
> | **[Reactant] Decreased** | Shift to Left (replace reactant) | [Product] Decreased |
> | **[Product] Increased** | Shift to Left (decrease product) | [Reactant] Increased |
> | **[Product] Decreased** | Shift to Right (replace product) | [Reactant] Decreased |

# 3.5 Solubility Equilibrium

> [!definition]- Solubility Equilibrium
> The equilibrium between a slightly soluble solid and its dissolved ions in solution.

> [!compare]- Solubility Concepts
> > [!definition]- Solubility
> > The amount of substance that dissolves in a given amount of solvent to make a ||saturated|| solution.
> > * **Unit:** Grams per liter ($g\,L^{-1}$).
>
> > [!definition]- Molar Solubility ($s$)
> > The number of moles of solute dissolved in 1 liter of saturated solution.
> > * **Unit:** Moles per liter ($M$ or $mol\,L^{-1}$).

## The Solubility Product Constant ($K_{sp}$)

> [!formula]- General Expression
> For a slightly soluble salt $M_aX_b$:
> $\ce{M_aX_b(s) <=> aM^{b+}(aq) + bX^{a-}(aq)}$
> $K_{sp} = [M^{b+}]^a [X^{a-}]^b$
> > [!unit]- $K_{sp}$
> > Solubility product constant (Equilibrium constant for solubility).
>
> > [!unit]- $[M^{b+}], [X^{a-}]$
> > Molar concentrations of ions in a saturated solution.
>
> > [!info]- Note
> > The solid reactant is **omitted** because its concentration is constant.

> [!liststart]- Relationship between $K_{sp}$ and Solubility ($s$)
> > [!continue]- 1:1 Salt (e.g., AgCl)
> > $\ce{AgCl(s) <=> Ag+(aq) + Cl-(aq)}$
> > * **Equilibrium:** $[Ag^+] = s$, $[Cl^-] = s$
> > * **$K_{sp}$ Expression:** $K_{sp} = (s)(s) = s^2$
> > * **Solubility:** $s = \sqrt{K_{sp}}$
>
> > [!continue]- 1:2 Salt (e.g., $\ce{PbCl2}$ or $\ce{Ca(OH)2}$)
> > $\ce{Ca(OH)2(s) <=> Ca^{2+}(aq) + 2OH-(aq)}$
> > * **Equilibrium:** $[Ca^{2+}] = s$, $[OH^-] = 2s$
> > * **$K_{sp}$ Expression:** $K_{sp} = (s)(2s)^2 = 4s^3$
> > * **Solubility:** $s = \sqrt[3]{\frac{K_{sp}}{4}}$
>
> > [!continue]- 1:3 Salt (e.g., $\ce{Fe(OH)3}$)
> > $\ce{Fe(OH)3(s) <=> Fe^{3+}(aq) + 3OH-(aq)}$
> > * **Equilibrium:** $[Fe^{3+}] = s$, $[OH^-] = 3s$
> > * **$K_{sp}$ Expression:** $K_{sp} = (s)(3s)^3 = 27s^4$
> > * **Solubility:** $s = \sqrt[4]{\frac{K_{sp}}{27}}$
> 
> > [!tip]- The general formula:
> > $K_{sp}=[a\;s_{M}]^{a}[b\;s_{X}]^{b}$

> [!sq]- Exercise 3: Calculating Solubility from $K_{sp}$
> The $K_{sp}$ for solid $\ce{PbCl2}$ is $1.7 \times 10^{-4}\,M^3$. **IMPORTANT: CONTAIN NEW FORMULA**
> > [!continue]- (a) Determine the Molar Solubility ($s$)
> > **Step 1: Dissociation Equation**
> > $\ce{PbCl2(s) <=> Pb^{2+}(aq) + 2Cl-(aq)}$
> >
> > **Step 2: ICE Table Expression**
> > If $s$ moles of $\ce{PbCl2}$ dissolve:
> > $[\ce{Pb^{2+}}] = s$
> > $[\ce{Cl-}] = 2s$
> >
> > **Step 3: Solve for $s$**
> > $K_{sp} = [\ce{Pb^{2+}}][\ce{Cl-}]^2 = (s)(2s)^2 = 4s^3$
> > $1.7 \times 10^{-4} = 4s^3$
> > $s^3 = \frac{1.7 \times 10^{-4}}{4} = 4.25 \times 10^{-5}$
> > $s = \sqrt[3]{4.25 \times 10^{-5}}$
> > $\enclose{box}{s \approx 3.5 \times 10^{-2}\,mol\,dm^{-3}}$
>
> > [!continue]- (b) Determine the Solubility ($g\,dm^{-3}$)
> > **Formula:** Solubility = Molar Solubility $\times$ Molar Mass
> > Given Molar Mass ($MM$) of $\ce{PbCl2} \approx 278.2\,g\,mol^{-1}$.
> > Solubility $= (3.5 \times 10^{-2})\times(278.2)$
> > $\enclose{box}{\text{Solubility} \approx 9.74\,g\,dm^{-3}}$

> [!sq]- Sample Problem: Calculating $K_{sp}$ from Solubility
> The solubility of $\ce{Ag2S}$ at $25^\circ \text{C}$ is $1.24 \times 10^{-14}\,g\,L^{-1}$. Determine the $K_{sp}$.
> > [!continue]- Step 1: Calculate Molar Solubility ($s$)
> > $MM(\ce{Ag2S}) \approx 248\,g\,mol^{-1}$.
> > $s = \frac{1.24 \times 10^{-14}}{248} = 5.0 \times 10^{-17}\,mol\,L^{-1}$.
> >
> > **Step 2: Dissociation Equation**
> > $\ce{Ag2S(s) <=> 2Ag+(aq) + S^{2-}(aq)}$
> > Concentrations: $[\ce{Ag+}] = 2s$, $[\ce{S^{2-}}] = s$.
> >
> > **Step 3: Calculate $K_{sp}$**
> > $K_{sp} = [\ce{Ag+}]^2 [\ce{S^{2-}}] = (2s)^2(s) = 4s^3$
> > $K_{sp} = 4(5.0 \times 10^{-17})^3$
> > $K_{sp} = 4(1.25 \times 10^{-49})$
> > $\enclose{box}{K_{sp} = 5.0 \times 10^{-49}}$

# 3.6 The Common Ion Effect

> [!definition]- Common ions
> ions from an external source that are similar to the ions in equilibrium

> [!definition]- The Common Ion Effect
> The shift in equilibrium caused by the addition of a compound having an ion in **common** with the dissolved substance.
> * **Effect:** Adding a common ion $\color{orange}i$ the solubility of a slightly soluble salt. $\color{orange}ii$ the formation of precipitation.
> 
> > [!blank]- $\color{orange}i$
> > decreases
> 
> > [!blank]- $\color{orange}ii$
> > increase

> [!liststart]- Mechanism
> Consider: $\ce{Ag2SO4(s) <=> 2Ag+(aq) + SO4^{2-}(aq)}$
> 
> > [!continue]- Add Common Ion ($\ce{Ag+}$)
> > Adding $\ce{AgNO3}$ (very soluble) increases $[\ce{Ag+}]$.
> > * According to Le Chatelier's Principle, the system shifts to the ||left|| to consume the excess $\ce{Ag+}$.
> > * **Result:** More $\ce{Ag2SO4}$ precipitates out. Solubility ||decreases||.

## Predicting Precipitation ($Q_{sp}$ vs $K_{sp}$)

> [!compare]- Will a Precipitate Form?
> Compare the Ion Product ($Q_{sp}$) to the Solubility Product ($K_{sp}$).
> > [!continue]- $Q_{sp} < K_{sp}$ (Unsaturated)
> > No precipitate forms. More solid can dissolve.
>
> > [!continue]- $Q_{sp} = K_{sp}$ (Saturated)
> > Solution is at equilibrium. No change.
>
> > [!continue]- $Q_{sp} > K_{sp}$ (Supersaturated)
> > ||Precipitate forms|| until $Q_{sp} = K_{sp}$.

> [!sq]- Exercise 4: Will $\ce{PbSO4}$ Precipitate?
> If $0.10\,L$ of $0.003\,M\,\ce{Pb(NO3)2}$ is added to $0.40\,L$ of $0.005\,M\,\ce{Na2SO4}$.
> Given $K_{sp}(\ce{PbSO4}) = 1.6 \times 10^{-8}$.
> > [!continue]- Step 1: Calculate Total Volume
> > $V_{total} = 0.10 + 0.40 = 0.50\,L$.
>
> > [!continue]- Step 2: Calculate Initial Moles
> > Moles $\ce{Pb^{2+}} = 0.003 \times 0.10 = 3 \times 10^{-4}\,mol$.
> > Moles $\ce{SO4^{2-}} = 0.005 \times 0.40 = 2 \times 10^{-3}\,mol$.
>
> > [!continue]- Step 3: Calculate New Concentrations
> > $[\ce{Pb^{2+}}]_{new} = \frac{3 \times 10^{-4}}{0.50} = 6 \times 10^{-4}\,M$.
> > $[\ce{SO4^{2-}}]_{new} = \frac{2 \times 10^{-3}}{0.50} = 4 \times 10^{-3}\,M$.
>
> > [!continue]- Step 4: Calculate $Q_{sp}$ and Conclude
> > $Q_{sp} = [\ce{Pb^{2+}}][\ce{SO4^{2-}}]$
> > $Q_{sp} = (6 \times 10^{-4})(4 \times 10^{-3}) = 2.4 \times 10^{-6}$.
> >
> > **Compare:** $2.4 \times 10^{-6} > 1.6 \times 10^{-8}$.
> > Since $Q_{sp} > K_{sp}$, reaction shifts ||left||.
> > **Conclusion:** $\ce{PbSO4}$ ||will precipitate||.
