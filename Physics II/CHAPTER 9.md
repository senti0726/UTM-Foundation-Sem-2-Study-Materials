# 9.1 Thin Lenses: Ray Tracing and Equation

> [!definition]- Thin Lens
> A lens whose thickness is small compared to its radii of curvature.

> [!liststart]- Types of Lenses
> ![[EditHere - Page 3 Diagram of Converging and Diverging Lenses]]
> > [!continue]- Converging Lens (Convex)
> > ![[Pasted image 20260203031720.png]]
> > Thicker at the $\color{orange}i$ than at the $\color{orange}ii$.
> > > [!blank]- $\color{orange}i$
> > > center
> > 
> > > [!blank]- $\color{orange}ii$
> > > edges
> 
> > [!continue]- Diverging Lens (Concave)
> > ![[Pasted image 20260203031758.png]]
> > Thicker at the $\color{orange}i$ than at the $\color{orange}ii$.
> > > [!blank]- $\color{orange}i$
> > > edges
> > 
> > > [!blank]- $\color{orange}ii$
> > > center

> [!definition]- Optical Centre ($O$)
> The geometric center of the lens.

> [!definition]- Focal Point ($F$)
> > [!continue]- For Converging Lens
> > The point where incident parallel rays ||converge|| after passing through the lens.
> > * The focus is ||real||.
> 
> > [!continue]- For Diverging Lens
> > The point where incident parallel rays seem to ||diverge from|| after passing through the lens.
> > * The focus is ||virtual||.

> [!formula]- Focal Length, $f$
> The distance between the focal point ($F$) and the optical center ($O$).
> > [!unit]- $f$
> > Focal length in meters ($m$)

> [!formula]- Lens Power, $P$
> The inverse of the focal length.
> $P = \frac{1}{f}$
> > [!unit]- $P$
> > Power in Diopters ($D$ or $m^{-1}$)
> 
> > [!unit]- $f$
> > Focal length in meters ($m$)

## Thin Lens Equation and Magnification

> [!formula]- Thin Lens Equation
> Relates object distance, image distance, and focal length.
> $\frac{1}{d_o} + \frac{1}{d_i} = \frac{1}{f}$
> > [!unit]- $d_o$
> > Object distance in meters ($m$)
> 
> > [!unit]- $d_i$
> > Image distance in meters ($m$)
> 
> > [!unit]- $f$
> > Focal length in meters ($m$)

> [!formula]- Magnification, $m$
> $m = \frac{h_i}{h_o} = -\frac{d_i}{d_o}$
> > [!unit]- $m$
> > Magnification (dimensionless)
> 
> > [!unit]- $h_i$
> > Image height in meters ($m$)
> 
> > [!unit]- $h_o$
> > Object height in meters ($m$)
> 
> > [!unit]- $d_i$
> > Image distance in meters ($m$)
> 
> > [!unit]- $d_o$
> > Object distance in meters ($m$)

> [!table]- Sign Conventions
> | Quantity | Positive (+) | Negative (-) |
> | :--- | :--- | :--- |
> | **Focal Length ($f$)** | Converging Lens | Diverging Lens |
> | **Object Distance ($d_o$)** | Real object (same side as light enters) | Virtual object (opposite side) |
> | **Image Distance ($d_i$)** | Real image (opposite side to light source) | Virtual image (same side as light source) |
> | **Magnification ($m$)** | Upright image | Inverted image |

> [!sq]- Example 9-1: Image formed by converging lens
> What are (a) the position, and (b) the size, of the image of a $7.6\text{-}cm\text{-high}$ leaf placed $1.00\,m$ from a $+50.0\text{-}mm\text{-focal-length}$ camera lens?
> ![[EditHere - Page 19 Diagram of leaf and lens]]
> > [!continue]- $a)$ Position of the image ($d_i$).
> > **Given:**
> > $h_o = 7.6\,cm$
> > $d_o = 1.00\,m = 100\,cm$
> > $f = +50.0\,mm = +5.00\,cm$
> >
> > **Calculation:**
> > $\frac{1}{f} = \frac{1}{d_o} + \frac{1}{d_i}$
> > $\frac{1}{5.00} = \frac{1}{100} + \frac{1}{d_i}$
> > $\frac{1}{d_i} = \frac{1}{5} - \frac{1}{100} = \frac{20 - 1}{100} = \frac{19}{100}$
> > $d_i = \frac{100}{19}$
> > $\enclose{box}{d_i \approx +5.26\,cm}$
> > (Positive $d_i$ means the image is ||real|| and formed at the back of the lens).
>
> > [!continue]- $b)$ Size of the image ($h_i$).
> > **Calculation:**
> > $m = \frac{h_i}{h_o} = -\frac{d_i}{d_o}$
> > $\frac{h_i}{7.6} = -\frac{5.26}{100}$
> > $h_i = -0.0526 \times 7.6$
> > $\enclose{box}{h_i \approx -0.40\,cm}$
> > (Negative height means the image is ||inverted||).

> [!sq]- Example 9-2: Object close to converging lens
> An object is placed $10\,cm$ from a $15\text{-}cm\text{-focal-length}$ converging lens. Determine the image position and size.
> ![[EditHere - Page 21 Ray diagram for object inside f]]
> > [!continue]- Solution
> > **Given:**
> > $d_o = 10\,cm$
> > $f = +15\,cm$ (Converging)
> >
> > **Calculate Image Position:**
> > $\frac{1}{f} = \frac{1}{d_o} + \frac{1}{d_i}$
> > $\frac{1}{15} = \frac{1}{10} + \frac{1}{d_i}$
> > $\frac{1}{d_i} = \frac{1}{15} - \frac{1}{10} = \frac{2 - 3}{30} = -\frac{1}{30}$
> > $\enclose{box}{d_i = -30\,cm}$
> > (Negative sign indicates image is ||virtual|| and at the front of the lens).
> >
> > **Calculate Magnification:**
> > $m = -\frac{d_i}{d_o} = -\frac{-30}{10}$
> > $\enclose{box}{m = +3.0}$
> > (Positive means ||upright|| and magnified).

> [!sq]- Example 9-3: Diverging lens
> Where must a small insect be placed if a $25\text{-}cm\text{-focal-length}$ diverging lens is to form a virtual image $20\,cm$ from the lens, on the same side as the object?
> > [!continue]- Solution
> > **Given:**
> > $f = -25\,cm$ (Diverging)
> > $d_i = -20\,cm$ (Virtual, same side as object)
> >
> > **Calculate Object Distance:**
> > $\frac{1}{f} = \frac{1}{d_o} + \frac{1}{d_i}$
> > $\frac{1}{-25} = \frac{1}{d_o} + \frac{1}{-20}$
> > $\frac{1}{d_o} = \frac{1}{20} - \frac{1}{25} = \frac{5 - 4}{100} = \frac{1}{100}$
> > $\enclose{box}{d_o = 100\,cm}$

# 9.2 Combination of Lenses

> [!continue]- Principle of Operation
> In a system with multiple lenses, the image formed by the first lens becomes the $\color{orange}i$ for the second lens.
> > [!blank]- $\color{orange}i$
> > object
>
> > [!info]- Important Note on Distance
> > The object distance for the second lens is found by subtracting the image distance of the first lens from the separation distance between the lenses.
> > $d_{o2} = d - d_{i1}$

> [!formula]- Total Magnification, $M$
> The total magnification of a lens combination is the product of the magnifications of the individual lenses.
> $M = m_1 \times m_2 \times \dots$
> > [!unit]- $M$
> > Total magnification (dimensionless)
>
> > [!unit]- $m_1$
> > Magnification of the first lens
>
> > [!unit]- $m_2$
> > Magnification of the second lens

> [!sq]- Example 4: Compound Microscope
> The objective and eyepiece of a compound microscope are both converging lenses with focal lengths $f_o = 15.0\,mm$ and $f_e = 25.5\,mm$ respectively. A distance of $61.0\,mm$ separates the lenses. The microscope is used to examine a sample placed $24.1\,mm$ in front of the objective.
> ![[EditHere - Page 27 Microscope Diagram]]
> > [!continue]- $a)$ Determine the position of the final image.
> > **Given:**
> > $f_1 = 15.0\,mm$ (Objective)
> > $f_2 = 25.5\,mm$ (Eyepiece)
> > Separation $d = 61.0\,mm$
> > Object distance $d_{o1} = 24.1\,mm$
> >
> > **Step 1: Image from Lens 1 (Objective)**
> > $\frac{1}{f_1} = \frac{1}{d_{o1}} + \frac{1}{d_{i1}}$
> > $\frac{1}{15.0} = \frac{1}{24.1} + \frac{1}{d_{i1}}$
> > $\frac{1}{d_{i1}} = \frac{1}{15.0} - \frac{1}{24.1}$
> > $d_{i1} = 39.73\,mm$
> > (Image is ||real||, formed behind lens 1).
> > > [!unit]- $d_{i1}$
> > > Image distance from lens 1 in $mm$
> >
> > **Step 2: Object for Lens 2 (Eyepiece)**
> > The image from lens 1 becomes the object for lens 2.
> > $d_{o2} = d - d_{i1}$
> > $d_{o2} = 61.0 - 39.73$
> > $d_{o2} = 21.27\,mm$
> > > [!unit]- $d_{o2}$
> > > Object distance for lens 2 in $mm$
> >
> > **Step 3: Final Image from Lens 2**
> > $\frac{1}{f_2} = \frac{1}{d_{o2}} + \frac{1}{d_{i2}}$
> > $\frac{1}{25.5} = \frac{1}{21.27} + \frac{1}{d_{i2}}$
> > $\frac{1}{d_{i2}} = \frac{1}{25.5} - \frac{1}{21.27}$
> > $d_{i2} = -129.3\,mm$
> > $\enclose{box}{d_{i2} \approx -129\,mm}$
> > (Negative sign indicates the final image is ||virtual|| and formed in front of the eyepiece).
>
> > [!continue]- $b)$ Determine the overall magnification.
> > **Calculate $m_1$:**
> > $m_1 = -\frac{d_{i1}}{d_{o1}} = -\frac{39.73}{24.1} = -1.65$
> >
> > **Calculate $m_2$:**
> > $m_2 = -\frac{d_{i2}}{d_{o2}} = -\frac{-129.3}{21.27} = +6.08$
> >
> > **Calculate Total Magnification $M$:**
> > $M = m_1 \times m_2$
> > $M = (-1.65)(6.08)$
> > $\enclose{box}{M \approx -10.0}$
> > (Negative sign indicates the final image is ||inverted||).
>
> > [!continue]- $c)$ State the characteristics of the final image.
> > * ||Virtual||
> > * ||Magnified|| (10x larger)
> > * ||Inverted|| (relative to original object)
> > * Formed ||in front|| of the eyepiece (Lens 2)

> [!sq]- Example 9-4: A two-lens system
> Two converging lenses, A and B, with focal lengths $f_A = 20.0\,cm$ and $f_B = 25.0\,cm$, are placed $80.0\,cm$ apart. An object is placed $60.0\,cm$ in front of the first lens.
> ![[EditHere - Page 29 Diagram of two converging lenses]]
> > [!continue]- $a)$ Determine the position of the final image.
> > **Given:**
> > $f_A = 20.0\,cm$, $f_B = 25.0\,cm$
> > Separation $d = 80.0\,cm$
> > $d_{oA} = 60.0\,cm$
> >
> > **Step 1: Lens A**
> > $\frac{1}{20.0} = \frac{1}{60.0} + \frac{1}{d_{iA}}$
> > $\frac{1}{d_{iA}} = \frac{3}{60} - \frac{1}{60} = \frac{2}{60}$
> > $d_{iA} = +30.0\,cm$
> > (Real image).
> >
> > **Step 2: Object for Lens B**
> > $d_{oB} = d - d_{iA}$
> > $d_{oB} = 80.0 - 30.0$
> > $d_{oB} = 50.0\,cm$
> >
> > **Step 3: Lens B**
> > $\frac{1}{25.0} = \frac{1}{50.0} + \frac{1}{d_{iB}}$
> > $\frac{1}{d_{iB}} = \frac{2}{50} - \frac{1}{50} = \frac{1}{50}$
> > $\enclose{box}{d_{iB} = +50.0\,cm}$
> > (Positive sign means final image is ||real|| and formed ||behind|| Lens B).
>
> > [!continue]- $b)$ Determine the total magnification.
> > **Lens A:**
> > $m_A = -\frac{d_{iA}}{d_{oA}} = -\frac{30.0}{60.0} = -0.5$
> >
> > **Lens B:**
> > $m_B = -\frac{d_{iB}}{d_{oB}} = -\frac{50.0}{50.0} = -1.0$
> >
> > **Total Magnification:**
> > $M = m_A \times m_B$
> > $M = (-0.5)(-1.0)$
> > $\enclose{box}{M = +0.5}$
> > (Positive means ||upright||; magnitude < 1 means ||diminished||).

# 9.3 Lensmaker's Equation

> [!continue]- Purpose
> This useful equation relates the $\color{orange}i$ of the two lens surfaces, and the $\color{orange}ii$, to the focal length.
> > [!blank]- $\color{orange}i$
> > radii of curvature ($R_1, R_2$)
>
> > [!blank]- $\color{orange}ii$
> > index of refraction ($n$)

> [!formula]- Lensmaker's Equation
> $\frac{1}{f} = (n_{lens} - 1)\left(\frac{1}{R_1} + \frac{1}{R_2}\right)$
> > [!formula]- General Form (in different medium)
> > If the lens is in a medium other than air (e.g., water):
> > $\frac{1}{f} = \left(\frac{n_{lens}}{n_{medium}} - 1\right)\left(\frac{1}{R_1} + \frac{1}{R_2}\right)$
>
> > [!unit]- $f$
> > Focal length in meters ($m$)
>
> > [!unit]- $n_{lens}$
> > Refractive index of the lens material
>
> > [!unit]- $n_{medium}$
> > Refractive index of the surrounding medium
>
> > [!unit]- $R_1$
> > Radius of curvature of the first surface in meters ($m$)
>
> > [!unit]- $R_2$
> > Radius of curvature of the second surface in meters ($m$)

> [!liststart]- Sign Convention for Radius of Curvature ($R$)
> ![[EditHere - Page 31 Diagram of lens surfaces]]
> > [!continue]- Convex Surface
> > Radius is ||positive (+)||.
>
> > [!continue]- Concave Surface
> > Radius is ||negative (-)||.
>
> > [!continue]- Flat (Planar) Surface
> > Radius is ||infinity ($\infty$)||.

> [!sq]- Example 6: Biconvex Lens (Camera)
> A camera uses a thin biconvex lens of radii of curvature $7.69\,mm$ made from crown glass ($n=1.52$).
> ![[EditHere - Page 32 Diagram of biconvex lens]]
> > [!continue]- $a)$ Calculate the focal length of the lens.
> > **Given:**
> > $n = 1.52$
> > Biconvex $\rightarrow$ Both surfaces are convex outward.
> > $R_1 = +7.69\,mm = +7.69 \times 10^{-3}\,m$
> > $R_2 = +7.69\,mm = +7.69 \times 10^{-3}\,m$
> >
> > **Calculation:**
> > $\frac{1}{f} = (n - 1)\left(\frac{1}{R_1} + \frac{1}{R_2}\right)$
> > $\frac{1}{f} = (1.52 - 1)\left(\frac{1}{7.69 \times 10^{-3}} + \frac{1}{7.69 \times 10^{-3}}\right)$
> > $\frac{1}{f} = (0.52)\left(\frac{2}{7.69 \times 10^{-3}}\right)$
> > $f = \frac{7.69 \times 10^{-3}}{1.04}$
> > $\enclose{box}{f = 7.39 \times 10^{-3}\,m = 7.39\,mm}$
> > > [!unit]- $f$
> > > Focal length in $m$
>
> > [!continue]- $b)$ Determine the image position and height.
> > **Given:**
> > Object height $h_o = 1.75\,m$
> > Object distance $d_o = 2.50\,m$
> > $f = 7.39 \times 10^{-3}\,m$
> >
> > **Find Image Distance ($d_i$):**
> > $\frac{1}{f} = \frac{1}{d_o} + \frac{1}{d_i}$
> > $\frac{1}{7.39 \times 10^{-3}} = \frac{1}{2.50} + \frac{1}{d_i}$
> > $135.3 = 0.4 + \frac{1}{d_i}$
> > $\frac{1}{d_i} \approx 134.9$
> > $\enclose{box}{d_i \approx 7.41 \times 10^{-3}\,m = 7.41\,mm}$
> > (Real image).
> >
> > **Find Image Height ($h_i$):**
> > $m = \frac{h_i}{h_o} = -\frac{d_i}{d_o}$
> > $\frac{h_i}{1.75} = -\frac{7.41 \times 10^{-3}}{2.50}$
> > $h_i = -2.96 \times 10^{-3} \times 1.75$
> > $\enclose{box}{h_i \approx -5.19 \times 10^{-3}\,m = -5.19\,mm}$
> > (Inverted).

> [!sq]- Example 7: Plano-Convex Lens
> A thin plano-convex lens is made of glass ($n=1.66$). When an object is set $10\,cm$ from the lens, a virtual image ten times its size is formed.
> > [!continue]- $a)$ Determine the focal length of the lens.
> > **Given:**
> > $d_o = 10\,cm$
> > $m = +10$ (Virtual image is upright)
> >
> > **Find Image Distance ($d_i$):**
> > $m = -\frac{d_i}{d_o} \implies 10 = -\frac{d_i}{10}$
> > $d_i = -100\,cm$
> >
> > **Find Focal Length ($f$):**
> > $\frac{1}{f} = \frac{1}{d_o} + \frac{1}{d_i}$
> > $\frac{1}{f} = \frac{1}{10} + \frac{1}{-100} = \frac{10 - 1}{100} = \frac{9}{100}$
> > $\enclose{box}{f = 11.11\,cm}$
>
> > [!continue]- $b)$ Determine the radius of curvature of the convex surface.
> > **Given:**
> > Plano-convex $\implies$ One surface flat ($R_2 = \infty$), one convex ($R_1 = ?$).
> > $n = 1.66$
> >
> > **Calculation:**
> > $\frac{1}{f} = (n - 1)\left(\frac{1}{R_1} + \frac{1}{R_2}\right)$
> > $\frac{1}{11.11} = (1.66 - 1)\left(\frac{1}{R_1} + \frac{1}{\infty}\right)$
> > $0.09 = (0.66)\left(\frac{1}{R_1} + 0\right)$
> > $R_1 = \frac{0.66}{0.09}$
> > $\enclose{box}{R_1 = 7.33\,cm}$

> [!sq]- Example 8: Concave Meniscus Lens
> The radii of curvature of a thin concave meniscus lens ($n=1.5$) are $20\,cm$ and $10\,cm$.
> ![[EditHere - Page 35 Diagram of meniscus]]
> > [!continue]- $a)$ Focal length in air.
> > **Sign Convention:**
> > Meniscus $\implies$ One convex, one concave.
> > Given it is "Concave Meniscus" (diverging dominant), the concave surface must have the stronger curve (smaller radius).
> > Let Surface 1 be Convex ($R_1 = +20\,cm$).
> > Let Surface 2 be Concave ($R_2 = -10\,cm$).
> >
> > **Calculation:**
> > $\frac{1}{f} = (1.5 - 1)\left(\frac{1}{20} + \frac{1}{-10}\right)$
> > $\frac{1}{f} = (0.5)\left(0.05 - 0.1\right)$
> > $\frac{1}{f} = (0.5)(-0.05) = -0.025$
> > $\enclose{box}{f = -40\,cm}$
> > (Negative $f$ confirms it is a diverging lens).
>
> > [!continue]- $b)$ Focal length in water ($n_w = 4/3$).
> > **Calculation:**
> > $\frac{1}{f'} = \left(\frac{n_{lens}}{n_{water}} - 1\right)\left(\frac{1}{R_1} + \frac{1}{R_2}\right)$
> > $\frac{1}{f'} = \left(\frac{1.5}{1.33} - 1\right)\left(\frac{1}{20} - \frac{1}{10}\right)$
> > $\frac{1}{f'} = (1.125 - 1)(-0.05)$
> > $\frac{1}{f'} = (0.125)(-0.05) = -0.00625$
> > $\enclose{box}{f' = -160\,cm}$

> [!sq]- Example 9-5: Convex Meniscus Lens
> A convex meniscus lens ($n=1.50$) has a convex surface ($R=22.4\,cm$) and a concave surface ($R=46.2\,cm$).
> ![[EditHere - Page 37 Diagram of convex meniscus]]
> > [!continue]- $a)$ What is the focal length?
> > **Sign Convention:**
> > Surface 1 (Convex): $R_1 = +22.4\,cm$
> > Surface 2 (Concave): $R_2 = -46.2\,cm$
> >
> > **Calculation:**
> > $\frac{1}{f} = (1.50 - 1)\left(\frac{1}{22.4} + \frac{1}{-46.2}\right)$
> > $\frac{1}{f} = 0.5(0.0446 - 0.0216)$
> > $\frac{1}{f} = 0.5(0.023)$
> > $f = \frac{1}{0.0115}$
> > $\enclose{box}{f \approx 87.0\,cm}$
> > (Positive $f$ indicates it is a ||converging|| lens).
>
> > [!continue]- $b)$ Where will the image be for an object $2.00\,m$ away?
> > **Given:**
> > $d_o = 2.00\,m = 200\,cm$
> > $f = 87.0\,cm$
> >
> > **Calculation:**
> > $\frac{1}{d_i} = \frac{1}{f} - \frac{1}{d_o}$
> > $\frac{1}{d_i} = \frac{1}{87.0} - \frac{1}{200}$
> > $\frac{1}{d_i} = 0.0115 - 0.005$
> > $\frac{1}{d_i} = 0.0065$
> > $\enclose{box}{d_i \approx 154\,cm = 1.54\,m}$
