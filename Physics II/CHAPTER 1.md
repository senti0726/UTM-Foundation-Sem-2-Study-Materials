# 1.2 Coulomb's Law
Experiment shows that the electric force between two charges is proportional to the product of the charges and inversely proportional to the distance between them
> [!formula]- Coulomb's Law
> $F_{e}=\frac{k_{e}Q_{1}Q_{2}}{r^{2}}$
> > [!unit]- $F$
> > magnitude of electrostatic force, $N$
> 
> > [!unit]- $Q_{1}Q_{2}$
> > magnitude of charges; unit: Coulomb
> 
> > [!unit]- $r$
> > distance between two point charges
> 
> > [!formula]- $k_{e}$
> > $k_{e}=\frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_{o}}$
> > > [!unit]- $k$ is Coulomb constant $=9.00\times 10^{9}N\,m^{2}\,C^{-2}$
> > 
> > > [!unit]- $\epsilon_{o}$
> > > permittivity of free space $=8.8542\times 10^{-12}C^{2}\,N^{-1}\,m^{-2}$

> [!unit]- Charge on the electron
> $e=1.602\times 10^{-19}C$
> Electric charge is quantised in units of the electron charge

> [!transparent]- Example 1.1: Three charges in a line
> Three charged particles are arranged in a line, as shown. Calculate the net electrostatic force on particle $3$ (the $-4.0\mu C$ on the right) due to the other two charges.
> $F=\frac{kQ_{1}Q_{2}}{r^{2}}$
> > [!tip]- net electrostatic force of $3$ due to $1$ and $2$, which means sum up $F_{31}$ and $F_{32}$.
> 
> $F_{31}=\frac{kQ_{3}Q_{1}}{r^{2}}$
> $=\frac{(8.99\times 10^{9})(-4\mu C)(-8\mu C)}{(0.3+0.2)^{2}}=1.15N$
> $F_{32}=\frac{kQ_{3}Q_{2}}{r^{2}}$
> $\frac{(8.99\times 10^{9})(-4\mu C)(3\mu C)}{0.2^{2}}=2.7N$
> $F=F_{31}+(-F_{32})$
> $=1.15-2.7$
> $=-1.55N$

# 1.3 The Electric Field
> [!definition]- The electric field strength at a point is defined as
> the electric (electrostatic) force per unit positive charge that acts at that point in the same direction as the force

> [!formula]- Electric field strength
> $\vec{E}=\frac{\vec{F}}{q_{o}}$
> > [!unit]- $\vec{E}$
> > Electric field strength in $N\,C^{-1}$ or $V\,m^{-1}$
> 
> > [!unit]- $\vec{F}$
> > electric force in $N$
> 
> > [!unit]- $q_{0}$
> > test charge in $C$

> [!formula]- Since $F=\frac{kQq_{0}}{r^{2}}$
> then the equation of electric field strength can be written as
> $\enclose{box}{E=\frac{kQ}{r^{2}}}$
> $E=\frac{\frac{kQq_{0}}{r^{2}}}{q_{0}}$
> sub $k=\frac{1}{4\pi \epsilon_{0}}$
> $\enclose{box}{E=\frac{Q}{4\pi\epsilon_{0}r^{2}}}$
> > [!unit]- $Q$
> > magnitude of a point charge
> 
> > [!unit]- $r$
> > distance between a point and point charge

> [!note]- $\underline{\text{Sample Question: 1.3}}$
> Calculate the magnitude and direction of the electric field at a point $P$ which is $30cm$ to the right of a point charge $Q=-3.0\times 10^{-6}C$
> > [!continue]- Solution
> > $r=30cm=0.3m$
> > $Q=-3.0\times 10^{-6}cm$
> > $E=\frac{kQ}{r^{2}}$
> > $=\frac{(8.99\times 10^{9})(3\times 10^{-6})}{0.3^{2}}$
> > $=3\times 10^{5}N\,C^{-1}$
> > direction of electric field of $P$ is toward $Q$, which is to the left, because $Q<0$ $P=pcs$ which is attractive.

## Electric potential
> [!info]- Symbol
> $V$

> [!definition]- Electric potential of a point in an electric field is defined as
> > [!continue]- In term of potential energy
> > the potential energy per unit positive charge at that point in the electric field
> 
> > [!continue]- In term of work done
> > the work done in bringing positive test charge from infinity to that point in the electric field per unit test charge.

> [!formula]- Electric potential
> > [!continue]- In term of potential energy
> > $V=\frac{U}{q_{0}}$
> > $\therefore\;V=k \frac{Q}{r}$
> 
> > [!continue]- In term of work done
> > $V=\frac{W_{\infty}}{q_{0}}$
> 
> > [!unit]- Units
> > > [!unit]- $q_{0}$
> > > value of a test charge (including sign)
> > 
> > > [!unit]- $V$
> > > $V$ / $J\,C^{-1}$
> > 
> > > [!continue]- Type of quantity
> > > scalar

## 1.4.1 Electrostatic Potential Energy
> [!definition]- Electric potential energy of a charge $q$ at a point in an electric field is defined as
> the work done to bring the charge from infinity to the point.

> [!info]- Symbol
> $U$

> [!formula]- If $V$ is the electric potential at that point, then the electric potential energy $U$ of a charge $q$ at that point is
> $U=qV=k \frac{Qq}{r}$
> > [!unit]- $U$
> > Electric potential energy in $J$
> 
> > [!unit]- $q$
> > Magnitude of the charge placed at the point in $C$
> 
> > [!unit]- $V$
> > Electric potential at that point in $V$
> 
> > [!unit]- $k$
> > Coulomb's constant $(9.0\times 10^{9}N\,m^{2}\,C^{-2})$
> 
> > [!unit]- $Q$
> > Source charge creating the potential in $C$
> 
> > [!unit]- $r$
> > Distance between charges $Q$ and $q$ in $m$

## 1.4.2 Electric Potential Difference
> [!definition]- The electric potential difference between two points $A$ and $B$ in an electric field is defined as
> the work to bring a unit positive charge from one point to another

> [!formula]- Electric Potential Difference in terms of Work and Energy
> $V_{ba}=V_{b}-V_{a}=\frac{U_{b}-U_{a}}{q}=-\frac{W_{ba}}{q}$
> $W_{ba}=-q\Delta V_{ba}$
> > [!unit]- $V_{ba}$
> > Potential difference between point $b$ and $a$ in $V$ or $J\,C^{-1}$
> 
> > [!unit]- $U_{b}-U_{a}$
> > Change in electric potential energy in $J$
> 
> > [!unit]- $q$
> > Magnitude of the charge moved in $C$
> 
> > [!unit]- $W_{ba}$
> > Work done by the electric force to move charge $q$ from $a$ to $b$ in $J$
> 
> > [!unit]- $\Delta V_{ba}$
> > Alternative notation for potential difference ($V_{b}-V_{a}$) in $V$

> > $r_{T}=10\times 10^{-2}m$

> > $V_{ST}=V_{S}-V_{T}$

## 1.4.3 Relation between Electric Potential and Electric Field
> [!formula]- Relation between electric potential and electric field
> > [!formula]- The general relationship between a conservative force and potential energy
> > $U_{b}-U_{a}=-\int ^{b}_{a}\vec{F}\cdot d\vec{\ell}$
> 
> > [!formula]- Substituting the potential difference and the electric field
> > $V_{ba}=V_{b}-V_{a}=-\int ^{b}_{a}\vec{E}\cdot d\vec{\ell}$
> 
> > [!formula]- The simplest case is a uniform field
> > $V_{ba}=-Ed$
> > > [!unit]- $V_{ba}$
> > > Potential difference ($V_b - V_a$) in $V$ (Volts)
> > 
> > > [!unit]- $E$
> > > Magnitude of the electric field strength in $V\,m^{-1}$ or $N\,C^{-1}$
> > 
> > > [!unit]- $d$
> > > Distance between points $a$ and $b$ parallel to the field lines in $m$
> > 
> > > [!unit]- $-$
> > > Indicates that potential decreases in the direction of the electric field

> [!note]- $\underline{\text{Sample Question}}$
