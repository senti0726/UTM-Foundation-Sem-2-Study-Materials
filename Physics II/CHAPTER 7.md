# 7.1 Electromagnetic Waves

> [!continue]- Production of EM Waves
> Since a changing $\color{orange}i$ produces $\color{orange}ii$, and a changing $\color{orange}ii$ produces $\color{orange}i$, once $\color{orange}iii$ are created they can $\color{orange}iv$. These $\color{orange}v$ are called $\color{orange}vi$
> > [!blank]- $\color{orange}i$
> > electric field
> 
> > [!blank]- $\color{orange}ii$
> > magnetic field
> 
> > [!blank]- $\color{orange}iii$
> > sinusoidal fields
> 
> > [!blank]- $\color{orange}iv$
> > propagate on their own
> 
> > [!blank]- $\color{orange}v$
> > propagating fields
> 
> > [!blank]- $\color{orange}vi$
> > electromagnetic waves

> [!liststart]- Characteristics of EM Waves
> ![[Pasted image 20260202204357.png]]
> > [!continue]- Orientation
> > The electric ($\vec{E}$) and magnetic ($\vec{B}$) waves are perpendicular to each other, and to the direction of propagation.
> 
> > [!continue]- Direction of Motion
> > The entire pattern moves in a direction perpendicular to both $\vec{E}$ and $\vec{B}$.

> [!formula]- Mathematical Representation
> For a wave propagating in the $x$-direction (or $z$-direction as per some texts), the fields are given by:
> $E = E_0 \sin(kx - \omega t)$
> $B = B_0 \sin(kx - \omega t)$
> > [!unit]- $E$
> > Electric field strength at position $x$ and time $t$ in $V\,m^{-1}$
> 
> > [!unit]- $B$
> > Magnetic field strength at position $x$ and time $t$ in $T$
> 
> > [!unit]- $E_0, B_0$
> > Amplitude (peak value) of electric and magnetic fields
> 
> > [!unit]- $k$
> > Wavenumber ($k = \frac{2\pi}{\lambda}$) in $m^{-1}$
> 
> > [!unit]- $\omega$
> > Angular frequency ($\omega = 2\pi f$) in $rad\,s^{-1}$
> 
> > [!unit]- $t$
> > Time in $s$
> 
> > [!unit]- $x$
> > Position along the direction of propagation in $m$

> [!formula]- Wave Speed Relationships
> $f\lambda = \frac{\omega}{k} = v$
> $\frac{E}{B} = v$
> > [!unit]- $f$
> > Frequency in $Hz$
> 
> > [!unit]- $\lambda$
> > Wavelength in $m$
> 
> > [!unit]- $v$
> > Speed of the wave in $m\,s^{-1}$

# 7.2 EM Waves and Their Speed

> [!formula]- Speed of Light, $c$
> Electromagnetic waves travel at the speed of light in a vacuum.
> $v = c = \frac{1}{\sqrt{\epsilon_0 \mu_0}}$
> $c = 3.0 \times 10^8\,m\,s^{-1}$
> > [!unit]- $c$
> > Speed of light in vacuum
> 
> > [!unit]- $\epsilon_0$
> > Permittivity of free space ($8.85 \times 10^{-12}\,F\,m^{-1}$)
> 
> > [!unit]- $\mu_0$
> > Permeability of free space ($4\pi \times 10^{-7}\,H\,m^{-1}$)

> [!formula]- Frequency and Wavelength Relation
> $c = f \lambda$
> > [!unit]- $c$
> > speed of light in $3.0\times 10^{8}m\,s^{-1}$
> 
> > [!unit]- $f$
> > Frequency in $Hz$
> 
> > [!unit]- $\lambda$
> > Wavelength in $m$

> [!sq]- Example 31-2: Determining $\vec{E}$ and $\vec{B}$ in EM waves
> Assume a $60\text{-}Hz$ EM wave is a sinusoidal wave propagating in the $z$ direction with $\vec{E}$ pointing in the $x$ direction, and $E_0 = 2.0\,V/m$. Write vector expressions for $\vec{E}$ and $\vec{B}$ as functions of position and time.
> > [!continue]- Solution
> > **Given:**
> > $f = 60\,Hz$
> > Propagation: $z$-direction
> > $\vec{E}$ direction: $x$-direction
> > $E_0 = 2.0\,V\,m^{-1}$
> >
> > **Calculate Angular Frequency $\omega$:**
> > $\omega = 2\pi f = 2\pi(60)$
> > $\omega = 120\pi\,rad\,s^{-1} \approx 377\,rad\,s^{-1}$
> >
> > **Calculate Wavelength $\lambda$ and Wavenumber $k$:**
> > $c = f\lambda \implies \lambda = \frac{c}{f} = \frac{3.0 \times 10^8}{60} = 5.0 \times 10^6\,m$
> > $k = \frac{2\pi}{\lambda} = \frac{2\pi}{5.0 \times 10^6}$
> > $k = 1.26 \times 10^{-6}\,m^{-1}$
> >
> > **Calculate Magnetic Field Amplitude $B_0$:**
> > $\frac{E_0}{B_0} = c \implies B_0 = \frac{E_0}{c}$
> > $B_0 = \frac{2.0}{3.0 \times 10^8}$
> > $B_0 = 6.67 \times 10^{-9}\,T$
> >
> > **Determine Direction of $\vec{B}$:**
> > Propagation is $\vec{E} \times \vec{B}$.
> > $\hat{i} (\text{for } E) \times \hat{j} (\text{for } B) = \hat{k} (\text{for } z)$.
> > Thus $\vec{B}$ is in the $y$-direction.
> >
> > **Expressions:**
> > $\enclose{box}{E_x = 2.0 \sin(1.26 \times 10^{-6}z - 120\pi t)\,V\,m^{-1}}$
> > $\enclose{box}{B_y = 6.67 \times 10^{-9} \sin(1.26 \times 10^{-6}z - 120\pi t)\,T}$

> [!sq]- Example 31-3: Wavelengths of EM waves
> Calculate the wavelength of:
> > [!continue]- $a)$ A $60\text{-}Hz$ EM wave.
> > $c = f\lambda \implies \lambda = \frac{c}{f}$
> > $\lambda = \frac{3.0 \times 10^8}{60}$
> > $\enclose{box}{\lambda = 5.0 \times 10^6\,m}$
>
> > [!continue]- $b)$ A $93.3\text{-}MHz$ FM radio wave.
> > $f = 93.3 \times 10^6\,Hz$
> > $\lambda = \frac{3.0 \times 10^8}{93.3 \times 10^6}$
> > $\enclose{box}{\lambda = 3.22\,m}$
>
> > [!continue]- $c)$ A beam of visible red light from a laser at frequency $4.74 \times 10^{14}\,Hz$.
> > $\lambda = \frac{3.0 \times 10^8}{4.74 \times 10^{14}}$
> > $\enclose{box}{\lambda = 6.33 \times 10^{-7}\,m = 633\,nm}$

> [!sq]- Example 31-4: Cell phone antenna
> The antenna of a cell phone is often $\frac{1}{4}$ wavelength long. A particular cell phone has an $8.5\text{-}cm\text{-long}$ straight rod for its antenna. Estimate the operating frequency of this phone.
> > [!continue]- Solution
> > Length $l = 8.5\,cm = 0.085\,m$.
> > Condition: $l = \frac{1}{4}\lambda$
> > $\lambda = 4l = 4(0.085) = 0.34\,m$
> >
> > Calculate Frequency:
> > $c = f\lambda \implies f = \frac{c}{\lambda}$
> > $f = \frac{3.0 \times 10^8}{0.34}$
> > $f = 8.82 \times 10^8\,Hz$
> > $\enclose{box}{f \approx 880\,MHz}$
