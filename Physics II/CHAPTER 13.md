# 13.1 Nuclear Reactions and the Transmutation of Elements

> [!definition]- Nuclear Reaction
> A process that takes place when a nucleus is struck by another nucleus or particle.
> * If the original nucleus is transformed into another, this is called ||transmutation||.
> > [!example]- $^{4}_{2}\ce{He}+^{14}_{7}\ce{N}\to ^{17}_{8}\ce{O}+^{1}_{1}H$

> [!formula]- Generic Reaction Equation
> $a + X \rightarrow Y + b$
> * **a**: ||Projectile particle||
> * **X**: ||Target nucleus||
> * **Y**: ||Product nucleus|| (Daughter)
> * **b**: ||Ejected particle||

> [!liststart]- Conservation Laws
> In any nuclear reaction, the following must be conserved:
> > [!continue]- 1. Proton Number ($Z$)
> > The sum of protons before reaction = sum of protons after reaction.
> 
> > [!continue]- 2. Nucleon Number ($A$)
> > The sum of nucleons (protons + neutrons) before reaction = sum of nucleons after reaction.
> 
> > [!continue]- 3. Energy and Momentum
> > Total energy (including mass energy) and momentum are conserved.

## Reaction Energy ($Q$-value)

> [!definition]- Reaction Energy ($Q$)
> The sum of the initial masses less the sum of the final masses, multiplied by $c^2$.
> * It represents the energy released or absorbed during the reaction.

> [!continue]- Energy is released in a nuclear reaction in the form of
> kinetic energy of the particle emitted, the kinetic energy of the daughter nucleus and the energy of the gamma-ray photon that may accompany the reaction.

> [!formula]- Q-value Equation
> $Q = (M_{initial} - M_{final})c^2$
> $Q = (M_a + M_X - M_b - M_Y)c^2$
> > [!unit]- $Q$
> > Reaction energy in Joules ($J$) or $MeV$
> 
> > [!unit]- $M_a$
> > Mass of projectile
> 
> > [!unit]- $M_X$
> > Mass of target nucleus
> 
> > [!unit]- $M_b$
> > Mass of ejected particle
> 
> > [!unit]- $M_Y$
> > Mass of product nucleus
> 
> > [!unit]- $c$
> > Speed of light

> [!liststart]- Types of Reactions based on Q\
> > [!continue]- Exothermic Reaction ($Q > 0$)
> > * Mass of products is ||less|| than mass of reactants ($\Delta m > 0$).
> > * Energy is ||released|| (usually as kinetic energy).
> > * Occurs spontaneously.
> 
> > [!continue]- Endothermic Reaction ($Q < 0$)
> > * Mass of products is ||greater|| than mass of reactants ($\Delta m < 0$).
> > * Energy input is ||required|| to make the reaction happen.
> > * The incoming particle must have a minimum kinetic energy called the ||threshold energy||.

> [!formula]- Because energy is conserved, $Q$ has to be equal to the change in kinetic energy
> $Q=(K_{Y}+K_{b})-(K_{X}+K_{a})$

> [!formula]- Threshold Energy ($K_{min}$)
> For an endothermic reaction, the minimum kinetic energy the projectile must have is:
> $K_{min} = \left( 1 + \frac{m_a}{M_X} \right) |Q|$
> > [!unit]- $K_{min}$
> > Threshold kinetic energy in $MeV$
> 
> > [!unit]- $m_a$
> > Mass of projectile
> 
> > [!unit]- $M_X$
> > Mass of target nucleus
> 
> > [!unit]- $Q$
> > Reaction energy (magnitude)

> [!sq]- Example 13-1: Proton on Lithium
> A proton strikes a ${}_3^7Li$ nucleus, producing two $\alpha$ particles.
> ${}_1^1p + {}_3^7Li \rightarrow {}_2^4He + {}_2^4He$
> Given masses: $m_p = 1.007825\,u$, $m_{Li} = 7.016004\,u$, $m_{He} = 4.002603\,u$.
> > [!continue]- $a)$ Is the reaction exothermic or endothermic?
> > **Calculate Mass Difference:**
> > Initial Mass ($M_i$) = $1.007825 + 7.016004 = 8.023829\,u$
> > Final Mass ($M_f$) = $2 \times 4.002603 = 8.005206\,u$
> >
> > **Calculate Q:**
> > $Q = (M_i - M_f)c^2$
> > Mass defect $\Delta m = 8.023829 - 8.005206 = 0.018623\,u$
> > $Q = 0.018623 \times 931.5\,MeV$
> > $\enclose{box}{Q = 17.35\,MeV}$
> >
> > **Conclusion:**
> > Since $Q > 0$, the reaction is ||exothermic|| (energy is released).
>
> > [!continue]- $b)$ How much energy is released?
> > $\enclose{box}{17.35\,MeV}$

> [!sq]- Example 13-2: Neutron Capture
> A neutron strikes ${}_{5}^{10}B$ to produce ${}_{3}^{7}Li$ and an alpha particle.
> ${}_{0}^{1}n + {}_{5}^{10}B \rightarrow {}_{3}^{7}Li + {}_{2}^{4}He$
> Given: $m_n=1.008665\,u$, $m_B=10.012937\,u$, $m_{Li}=7.016004\,u$, $m_{He}=4.002603\,u$.
> > [!continue]- Calculate the energy released.
> > **Initial Mass:**
> > $M_i = 1.008665 + 10.012937 = 11.021602\,u$
> >
> > **Final Mass:**
> > $M_f = 7.016004 + 4.002603 = 11.018607\,u$
> >
> > **Calculate Q:**
> > $\Delta m = 11.021602 - 11.018607 = 0.002995\,u$
> > $Q = 0.002995 \times 931.5$
> > $\enclose{box}{Q \approx 2.79\,MeV}$

# 13.2 Nuclear Fission; Nuclear Reactors

> [!definition]- Nuclear Fission
> A nuclear reaction in which a heavy nucleus splits into two lighter nuclei (fission fragments) that are almost equal in mass, with the emission of neutrons and energy.
> * **Induced Fission:** Occurs when a heavy nucleus is bombarded by ||slow (thermal) neutrons||.
> * **Spontaneous Fission:** Occurs rarely without external trigger.

> [!continue]- Energy Release
> Fission releases significantly more energy than ||chemical reactions||.
> > [!because]- Energy is released because 
> > the average **binding energy per nucleon** of the fission products is ||greater|| than that of the parent nucleus. 

> [!sq]- Example Reaction: Uranium-235 Fission
> ${}_{92}^{235}U + {}_{0}^{1}n \rightarrow {}_{92}^{236}U^* \rightarrow X_1 + X_2 + \text{neutrons} + Q$
> A typical reaction:
> ${}_{92}^{235}U + {}_{0}^{1}n \rightarrow {}_{36}^{89}Kr + {}_{56}^{144}Ba + 3{}_{0}^{1}n + Q$
> * **Intermediate:** Excited ${}^{236}U$ nucleus.
> * **Products:** Krypton-89, Barium-144, and 3 neutrons.

> [!graph]- The mass distribution of the fission fragments shows that 
> the two pieces are large, but usually unequal
> ![[Pasted image 20260203211414.png]]

> [!sq]- Example 4: Energy from Uranium Fission
> Calculate the energy released when $20\,kg$ of Uranium-235 undergoes fission.
> Reaction: ${}^{235}U + n \rightarrow {}^{89}Kr + {}^{144}Ba + 3n$
> Given: $M(^{235}U)=235.04393\,u$, $M(^{89}Kr)=88.91756\,u$, $M(^{144}Ba)=143.92273\,u$, $m_n=1.00867\,u$.
> > [!continue]- $a)$ Calculate Q per single fission.
> > **Mass Defect ($\Delta m$):**
> > $\Delta m = (m_U + m_n) - (m_{Kr} + m_{Ba} + 3m_n)$
> > $\Delta m = m_U - m_{Kr} - m_{Ba} - 2m_n$
> > $\Delta m = 235.04393 - 88.91756 - 143.92273 - 2(1.00867)$
> > $\Delta m = 0.1863\,u$
> >
> > **Energy ($Q$):**
> > $Q = 0.1863 \times 931.5\,MeV$
> > $\enclose{box}{Q \approx 174\,MeV}$ (Slide calc: $173.96\,MeV$)
>
> > [!continue]- $b)$ Calculate total energy for 20 kg.
> > **Number of Nuclei ($N$):**
> > $N = \frac{\text{Mass}}{\text{Molar Mass}} \times N_A$
> > $N = \frac{20,000\,g}{235\,g/mol} \times 6.02 \times 10^{23}$
> > $N \approx 5.12 \times 10^{25}\,\text{nuclei}$
> >
> > **Total Energy:**
> > $E_{total} = N \times Q = (5.12 \times 10^{25})(174\,MeV)$
> > $\enclose{box}{E_{total} \approx 8.91 \times 10^{27}\,MeV}$

> [!definition]- Chain Reaction
> Neutrons released from one fission event can induce fission in other nuclei.
> * **Self-sustaining:** If at least one neutron from each fission triggers another fission.
> * **Critical Mass:** The minimum mass of fuel required to sustain a chain reaction (prevents too many neutrons from escaping).

> [!liststart]- Nuclear Reactors (Controlled Fission)
> To harness energy, the chain reaction must be controlled.
> > [!continue]- Moderator
> > Material (e.g., heavy water, graphite) used to ||slow down|| neutrons.
> > * **Reason:** Fission is most efficient with ||slow (thermal)|| neutrons.
> 
> > [!continue]- Fuel Enrichment
> > Natural Uranium contains only ~0.7% fissionable ${}^{235}U$.
> > * For reactors (using light water moderator), fuel must be **enriched** to ||2-3%|| ${}^{235}U$.
> 
> > [!continue]- Control Rods
> > Materials (like Cadmium or Boron) that absorb neutrons to control the reaction rate.

# 13.3 Nuclear Fusion

> [!definition]- Nuclear Fusion
> A type of nuclear reaction in which two light nuclei fuse to form a heavier nucleus with the release of large amounts of $\color{orange}i$.
> * The energy released is called $\color{orange}ii$ energy.
> 
> > [!blank]- $\color{orange}i$
> > energy
> 
> > [!blank]- $\color{orange}ii$
> > thermonuclear

> [!example]- Examples of fusion reaction releases the energy
> $^{2}_{1}\ce{H}+^{2}_{1}\ce{H}\to ^{3}_{2}\ce{He}+^{1}_{0}n+Q$
> $^{2}_{1}\ce{H}+^{2}_{1}\ce{H}\to ^{3}_{1}\ce{H}+^{1}_{1}\ce{H}+Q$

> [!continue]- Energy Source
> > [!graph]- Binding Energy Curve
> > ![[Pasted image 20260203212455.png]]
> 
> * For light nuclei ($A < 56$), the **binding energy per nucleon** $\color{orange}i$ as mass number increases.
> * When light nuclei fuse, the product is more tightly bound (more stable), so mass is lost and energy is $\color{orange}ii$.
> 
> > [!blank]- $\color{orange}i$
> > increases
> 
> > [!blank]- $\color{orange}ii$
> > released

## Fusion Reactions

> [!sq]- Example 13-3: Formation of Deuterium
> One of the simplest fusion reactions involves the production of deuterium (${}_{1}^{2}H$) from a neutron and a proton.
> ${}_{1}^{1}H + n \rightarrow {}_{1}^{2}H + \gamma$
> Given: $m_p = 1.007825\,u$, $m_n = 1.008665\,u$, $m_D = 2.014102\,u$.
> > [!continue]- How much energy is released?
> > **Step 1: Mass Difference ($\Delta m$)**
> > $\Delta m = (m_p + m_n) - m_D$
> > $\Delta m = (1.007825 + 1.008665) - 2.014102$
> > $\Delta m = 2.016490 - 2.014102 = 0.002388\,u$
> > (Slide calculation uses different rounding: $2.408 \times 10^{-3}\,u$)
> > Let's use slide value: $\Delta m = 2.408 \times 10^{-3}\,u$.
> >
> > **Step 2: Energy Released ($Q$)**
> > $Q = 2.408 \times 10^{-3} \times 931.5\,MeV$
> > $\enclose{box}{Q \approx 2.22\,MeV}$

> [!liststart]- Solar Fusion (Proton-Proton Chain)
> The Sun creates power by fusing hydrogen into helium.
> > [!continue]- Step 1
> > ${}_{1}^{1}H + {}_{1}^{1}H \rightarrow {}_{1}^{2}H + e^+ + \nu$
> > (Release: $0.44\,MeV$)
> 
> > [!continue]- Step 2
> > ${}_{1}^{1}H + {}_{1}^{2}H \rightarrow {}_{2}^{3}He + \gamma$
> > (Release: $5.48\,MeV$)
> 
> > [!continue]- Step 3
> > ${}_{2}^{3}He + {}_{2}^{3}He \rightarrow {}_{2}^{4}He + {}_{1}^{1}H + {}_{1}^{1}H$
> > (Release: $12.86\,MeV$)
> 
> > [!continue]- Net Effect
> > $4{}_{1}^{1}H \rightarrow {}_{2}^{4}He + 2e^+ + 2\nu + 2\gamma$

> [!sq]- Example 5: D-D Fusion
> Reaction: ${}_{1}^{2}H + {}_{1}^{2}H \rightarrow {}_{1}^{3}H + {}_{1}^{1}H$
> Given: $m_p = 1.007825\,u$, $m_T = 3.016049\,u$, $m_D = 2.014102\,u$.
> > [!continue]- $a)$ Energy released per reaction.
> > $\Delta m = 2(m_D) - (m_T + m_p)$
> > $\Delta m = 2(2.014102) - (3.016049 + 1.007825)$
> > $\Delta m = 4.028204 - 4.023874 = 0.00433\,u$
> > $Q = 0.00433 \times 931.5$
> > $\enclose{box}{Q \approx 4.03\,MeV}$
>
> > [!continue]- $b)$ Energy from 1.0 kg of Deuterium.
> > **Number of Nuclei:**
> > 1 mole ($2\,g$) contains $6.02 \times 10^{23}$ nuclei.
> > $1000\,g$ contains $\frac{1000}{2} \times N_A = 500 \times 6.02 \times 10^{23} = 3.01 \times 10^{26}$ nuclei.
> >
> > **Number of Reactions:**
> > It takes **2** nuclei to make 1 reaction.
> > Reactions $= \frac{3.01 \times 10^{26}}{2} = 1.505 \times 10^{26}$.
> >
> > **Total Energy:**
> > $E = (1.505 \times 10^{26})(4.03\,MeV)$
> > $\enclose{box}{E \approx 6.06 \times 10^{26}\,MeV}$

> [!sq]- Example 13-4: D-D Fusion (Helium Production)
> Reaction: ${}_{1}^{2}H + {}_{1}^{2}H \rightarrow {}_{2}^{4}He$
> Given: $m_D = 2.014102\,u$, $m_{He} = 4.002603\,u$.
> > [!continue]- Estimate energy released.
> > $\Delta m = 2(2.014102) - 4.002603$
> > $\Delta m = 4.028204 - 4.002603 = 0.025601\,u$
> > $Q = 0.025601 \times 931.5$
> > $\enclose{box}{Q \approx 23.85\,MeV}$

## Comparison: Fission vs Fusion
> [!liststart]- There are three fusion reactions that are being considered for power reactors
> $^{2}_{1}\ce{H}+^{2}_{1}\ce{H}\to^{3}_{1}\ce{H}+^{1}_{1}\ce{H}$
> $^{2}_{1}\ce{H}+^{2}_{1}\ce{H}\to ^{3}_{2}\ce{He}+n$
> $^{2}_{1}\ce{H}+^{3}_{1}\ce{H}\to ^{4}_{2}\ce{He}+n$
> These reaction use very common fuels, deuterium or tritium and release much more energy per nucleon than fission does.

> [!table]- Differences
> | Feature | Fission | Fusion |
> | :--- | :--- | :--- |
> | **Process** | Splitting a heavy nucleus into two smaller nuclei. | Combines two small nuclei to form a larger nucleus. |
> | **Temperature** | Occurs at controlled (moderate) temperatures. | Occurs at very high temperatures ($10^8 K$). |
> | **Control** | Easier to control and sustain (Chain reaction). | Difficult to control; sustained reaction not yet achieved for power. |
> | **Fuel** | Uranium, Plutonium (Radioactive, scarce). | Hydrogen isotopes (Deuterium is abundant in water). |
> | **Byproducts** | Highly radioactive waste. | Helium (Clean), though neutrons are produced. |

> [!liststart]- Fusion Reactors
> To achieve fusion, nuclei must have enough kinetic energy to overcome the electrical repulsion.
> > [!continue]- Confinement Methods
> > * **Magnetic Confinement (Tokamak):** Uses magnetic fields to contain hot plasma in a torus shape.
> > * **Inertial Confinement:** Uses powerful lasers to compress tiny fuel pellets.
