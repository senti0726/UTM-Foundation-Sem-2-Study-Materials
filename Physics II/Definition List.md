- Conductor

- Insulator
  a material in which almost no charge flows

  the electric (electrostatic) force per unit positive charge that acts at that point in the same direction as the force

- Electric potential energy of a charge $q$ at a point in an electric field is defined as

## CHAPTER 2
- Capacitor
  A device that is capable of storing electric charges or electric potential energy.
- Capacitance, $C$
  The ratio of the charge on either plate to the potential difference between them.
- 1 Farad is defined as
  > the charge of 1 coulomb stored on each of the conducting plates as a result of a potential difference of 1 volt between the two plates.
  
- Energy density
  the energy per unit volume
- Dielectric
  A non-conducting (insulating) material placed between the plates of a capacitor.
- Dielectric Constant (Relative Permittivity)
  The ratio between the capacitance of a given capacitor with dielectric ($C$) and the capacitance of the same capacitor in vacuum ($C_0$).
- Dielectric strength
  the electric field strength at which dielectric breakdown occurs and the material becomes a conductor

## CHAPTER 3
- Electric Battery
  A device that transforms $\color{orange}i$ into $\color{orange}ii$.
- Electric Current, $I$
  The rate of flow of charge through a conductor.
- 1 Ampere is defined as
  > $1\,A = 1\,C/s$ (1 coulomb of charge flowing per second)
  
- Ohm's Law
  States that the potential difference across a metallic conductor is proportional to the current flowing through it if its temperature is constant.
- Resistance, $R$
  The measure of a component's opposition to the flow of electric charge.
  Defined as the ratio of potential difference to the current.
- Resistivity, $\rho$
  The resistance of a unit cross-sectional area per unit length of the material.
  It is a measure of a material's ability to oppose the flow of an electric current.
- Electric Power, $P$
  The energy transformed by a device per unit time.

## CHAPTER 4
- Electromotive Force (emf), $\mathcal{E}$
  The energy provided by the source (battery/cell) to each unit charge that flows through the external and internal resistances.
- Terminal Potential Difference (Voltage), $V_{ab}$
  The work done in bringing a unit (test) charge from the negative to the positive terminals of the battery through the external resistance only.
- Kirchhoff's First Law (Junction Rule)
  The algebraic sum of the currents entering any junction in a circuit must equal the algebraic sum of the currents leaving that junction.
- Kirchhoff's Second Law (Loop Rule)
  The algebraic sum of the changes in potential around any closed loop is zero.
- Time Constant, $\tau$
  The quantity $RC$ is called the time constant. It measures how quickly the capacitor charges or discharges.
  $\tau = RC$

## CHAPTER 5
- Magnetic Field, $\vec{B}$
  A region around a magnet or moving charge where a magnetic force can be experienced.
- Ampere (Unit Definition)
  One ampere is defined as the current flowing in each of two long parallel wires $1\,m$ apart, which results in a force of exactly $2\times 10^{-7}\,N$ per meter of length of each wire.
- Solenoid
  is an electrical device in which a long wire has been wound into a succession of closely spaced loops with geometry of a helix.

## CHAPTER 6
- Magnetic Flux, $\Phi_B$
  A measure of the number of magnetic field lines passing through a surface area.
- Faraday's Law of Induction
  The emf induced in a circuit is equal to the rate of change of magnetic flux through the circuit.
- Lenz's Law
  An induced EMF is always in a direction that opposes the original change in flux that caused it.
  The current produced by an induced EMF moves in a direction such that the magnetic field produced tends to restore the changed field
- Electric Generator
  A device that transforms mechanical energy into electrical energy (the opposite of a motor).
- Transformer
  A device consisting of two coils (primary and secondary) linked by an iron core, used to change the magnitude of AC voltage.
- Self-Induction
  The process of producing an induced emf in a coil due to a change of current flowing through the same coil.
- Self-Inductance, $L$
  The ratio of the self-induced (back) emf to the rate of change of current in the coil.
  Also defined by magnetic flux linkage per unit current.
- Mutual inductance
  a changing current in one coil will induce a current in a second coil

## CHAPTER 11
- Blackbody
  An object that emits ||thermal radiation|| only (and absorbs all radiation falling on it, reflecting none).
- Electron-Volt ($eV$)
  A convenient unit of energy for atomic physics. It is defined as the kinetic energy gained by an electron in being accelerated by a potential difference of 1 volt.
- Photon
  A particle with $\color{orange}i$ mass consisting of a quantum of electromagnetic radiation where its energy is concentrated.
  * Travels at the speed of light ($c$).
  * Regarded as a unit of energy equal to ||$hf$||.
- Photoelectric Effect
  The emission of electrons (called **photoelectrons**) from the surface of a metal when electromagnetic radiation (light) of sufficiently high frequency strikes it.
  ![[Pasted image 20260203120625.png]]
- Photoelectron
  an electron emitted from the surface of the metal when the EM radiation (light) strikes its surface
- Work Function, $W_0$
  The minimum energy required to emit an electron from the surface of a specific metal.
- Principle of Complementarity
  Proposed by Niels Bohr. It states that both the wave and particle aspects of light are fundamental to its nature.
  * Light is ||dualistic|| in nature.
  * It behaves as a wave in some situations and as a particle in others.

## CHAPTER 12
- Isotopes
  Nuclides that have the same ||atomic number ($Z$)|| (same element) but different ||mass number ($A$)||.
  * This means they have the same number of ||protons|| but different number of ||neutrons||.
  
  **Examples:**
  * **Hydrogen:** ${}_{1}^{1}H$ (Protium), ${}_{1}^{2}H$ (Deuterium), ${}_{1}^{3}H$ (Tritium)
  * **Oxygen:** ${}_{8}^{16}O$, ${}_{8}^{17}O$, ${}_{8}^{18}O$
  
- Unified Atomic Mass Unit ($u$)
  Defined as 1/12th the mass of a Carbon-12 atom.
  $1\,u = 1.6605 \times 10^{-27}\,kg = 931.5\,MeV/c^2$
- Mass defect
  mass difference between the total mass of the constituent nucleons and the mass of a nucleus.
- Binding Energy
  The energy required to separate completely all the nucleons in the nucleus.
  * It is the energy equivalent of the ||mass defect||.
- Radioactivity
  The $\color{orange}i$ and $\color{orange}ii$ emission of radiation when an unstable nuclide decays.
- Disintegration energy
  The difference in mass between the parent nucleus with the sum of the daughter nucleus and the alpha particle
- Half-Life, $T_{1/2}$
  The time it takes for $\color{orange}i$ the nuclei in a given sample to decay.
  * It is related to the decay constant.

## CHAPTER 13
- Nuclear Reaction
  A process that takes place when a nucleus is struck by another nucleus or particle.
  * If the original nucleus is transformed into another, this is called ||transmutation||.
- Reaction Energy ($Q$)
  The sum of the initial masses less the sum of the final masses, multiplied by $c^2$.
  * It represents the energy released or absorbed during the reaction.
- Nuclear Fission
  A nuclear reaction in which a heavy nucleus splits into two lighter nuclei (fission fragments) that are almost equal in mass, with the emission of neutrons and energy.
  * **Induced Fission:** Occurs when a heavy nucleus is bombarded by ||slow (thermal) neutrons||.
  * **Spontaneous Fission:** Occurs rarely without external trigger.
- Chain Reaction
  Neutrons released from one fission event can induce fission in other nuclei.
  * **Self-sustaining:** If at least one neutron from each fission triggers another fission.
  * **Critical Mass:** The minimum mass of fuel required to sustain a chain reaction (prevents too many neutrons from escaping).
- Nuclear Fusion
  A type of nuclear reaction in which two light nuclei fuse to form a heavier nucleus with the release of large amounts of $\color{orange}i$.
  * The energy released is called $\color{orange}ii$ energy.
