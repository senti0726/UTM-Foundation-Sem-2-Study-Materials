# CHAPTER 1
- Coulomb's Law
  $F_{e}=\frac{k_{e}Q_{1}Q_{2}}{r^{2}}$
- $k_{e}$
  $k_{e}=\frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_{o}}$
- Electric field strength
  $\vec{E}=\frac{\vec{F}}{q_{o}}$
- Since $F=\frac{kQq_{0}}{r^{2}}$
  $\enclose{box}{E=\frac{kQ}{r^{2}}}$
  $E=\frac{\frac{kQq_{0}}{r^{2}}}{q_{0}}$
  sub $k=\frac{1}{4\pi \epsilon_{0}}$
  $\enclose{box}{E=\frac{Q}{4\pi\epsilon_{0}r^{2}}}$
- Relationship between electric field and surface of a conductor
- Electric potential
- Change in electric potential energy
  $U_{b}-U_{a}=-W=-qEd$
- General form of the potential due to a point charge:
  by setting the potential to zero at $r=\infty$
  $V=\frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_{0}} \frac{Q}{r},\;\left[ \text{single point charge; }V=0\text{ at }r=\infty \right]$
- If $V$ is the electric potential at that point, then the electric potential energy $U$ of a charge $q$ at that point is
  $U=qV=k \frac{Qq}{r}$
- Electric Potential Difference in terms of Work and Energy
  $V_{ba}=V_{b}-V_{a}=\frac{U_{b}-U_{a}}{q}=-\frac{W_{ba}}{q}$
  $W_{ba}=-q\Delta V_{ba}$
- Relation between electric potential and electric field
- The general relationship between a conservative force and potential energy
  $U_{b}-U_{a}=-\int ^{b}_{a}\vec{F}\cdot d\vec{\ell}$

- Substituting the potential difference and the electric field
  $V_{ba}=V_{b}-V_{a}=-\int ^{b}_{a}\vec{E}\cdot d\vec{\ell}$

- The simplest case is a uniform field
  $V_{ba}=-Ed$

# CHAPTER 2
- Capacitance Definition
  $C=\frac{Q}{V}$
  $Q=CV$
- Derivation
  $E=\frac{\sigma}{\epsilon_0} = \frac{Q}{A\epsilon_0}$
  $V=Ed = \frac{Qd}{A\epsilon_0}$
  $C=\frac{Q}{V} = \frac{Q}{\frac{Qd}{A\epsilon_0}}$
- Capacitance of Parallel-Plate Capacitor
  $C=\frac{\epsilon_{0}A}{d}$
- Equivalent capacitor in parallel
  $C_\text{eq}=C_{1}+C_{2}+C_{3}$
- Voltage
  $V = V_1 = V_2 = V_3$

- Charge
  $Q_{total} = Q_1 + Q_2 + Q_3$
- Effective Capacitance (Parallel)
  $C_{eff} = C_1 + C_2 + C_3 + \dots + C_n$
- Equivalent capacitance in series
- Charge
  $Q = Q_1 = Q_2 = Q_3$

- Voltage
  $V_{total} = V_1 + V_2 + V_3$
- Effective Capacitance (Series)
  $\frac{1}{C_{eff}} = \frac{1}{C_1} + \frac{1}{C_2} + \frac{1}{C_3} + \dots + \frac{1}{C_n}$
- Electric Potential Energy stored in Capacitor
  $U = \frac{1}{2}\frac{Q^2}{C} = \frac{1}{2}CV^2 = \frac{1}{2}QV$
- Energy Density
  $u = \frac{1}{2}\epsilon_0 E^2$
- Dielectric Constant and Permittivity
  $\epsilon_r = K = \frac{C}{C_0} =\frac{V_{0}}{V}= \frac{\epsilon}{\epsilon_0}=\frac{E_{0}}{E}$
- Capacitance with Dielectric
  $C = K \epsilon_0 \frac{A}{d} = \epsilon \frac{A}{d}$

- Current Formulas
- Average Current
  $I = \frac{Q}{t}$

- Instantaneous Current
  $I = \frac{dQ}{dt}$

- Conventional Current

- Electron Flow
- Ohm's Law
  $V \propto I \implies V = IR$
- Resistance Formula
  $R = \frac{V}{I}$
- Resistivity Formula
  $R = \frac{\rho l}{A} \implies \rho = \frac{RA}{l}$
- Temperature Dependence Formula
  $\rho_T = \rho_0 [1 + \alpha(T - T_0)]$
  $R_T = R_0 [1 + \alpha(T - T_0)]$
- Power Formula
  $P = \frac{dU}{dt} = \frac{dq}{dt}V = IV$
- Power in AC
  $P = I^2 R = I_0^2 R \sin^2 \omega t$
- Average Power, $\bar{P}$
  Since the average of $\sin^2 \omega t$ is $1/2$:
  $\bar{P} = \frac{1}{2}I_0^2 R$
  $\bar{P} = I_{rms}^2 R = \frac{V_{rms}^2}{R} = I_{rms}V_{rms}$
- RMS Formulas
  $I_{rms} = \sqrt{\bar{I^2}} = \frac{I_0}{\sqrt{2}} = 0.707 I_0$
  $V_{rms} = \sqrt{\bar{V^2}} = \frac{V_0}{\sqrt{2}} = 0.707 V_0$

# CHAPTER 5
- Ampere's Law
  $\oint \vec{B} \cdot d\vec{l} = \mu_0 I_{encl}$
- $a)$ Points outside the conductor ($r > R$)
  $\oint B \cdot dl = \mu_0 I_{encl}$
  $B(2\pi r) = \mu_0 I$
  $\enclose{box}{B = \frac{\mu_0 I}{2\pi r}}$

- $b)$ Points inside the conductor ($r < R$)
  Current density $J = \frac{I}{\pi R^2}$
  Enclosed current $I_{encl} = J(\pi r^2) = \frac{I}{\pi R^2}(\pi r^2) = I \frac{r^2}{R^2}$

  $B(2\pi r) = \mu_0 I \frac{r^2}{R^2}$
  $B = \frac{\mu_0 I r}{2\pi R^2}$
  $\enclose{box}{B \propto r}$

- Magnetic Field of a Long Straight Wire
  $B = \frac{\mu_0 I}{2\pi r}$
- Magnetic Field of a Solenoid
- Magnitude of magnetic field intensity at the centre (mid-point/inside) of $N$ turn solenoid
  $B = \frac{\mu_0 N I}{l} = \mu_0 n I$
- magnitude of magnetic field intensity at the end of $N$ turn solenoid is given by
  $B = \frac{1}{2}\mu_0 n I$
- Magnetic Force on a Wire
  $F = I l B \sin\theta$
- Vector Notation
  $\vec{F} = I(\vec{l} \times \vec{B})$

- Magnetic Force on a Moving Charge
  $\vec{F} = q(\vec{v} \times \vec{B})$
  $F = qvB \sin\theta$
- Radius of Circular Path
  Magnetic force provides centripetal force: $F_B = F_c$
  $qvB = \frac{mv^2}{r}$
  $\enclose{box}{r = \frac{mv}{Bq}}$
- Force per unit length
  $B_1 = \frac{\mu_0 I_1}{2\pi d}$ (Field from Wire 1 at Wire 2)
  $F_2 = I_2 l B_1 = I_2 l \left( \frac{\mu_0 I_1}{2\pi d} \right)$
  $\enclose{box}{\frac{F}{l} = \frac{\mu_0 I_1 I_2}{2\pi d}}$

# CHAPTER 6
- Magnetic Flux
  $\Phi_B = \int \vec{B} \cdot d\vec{A} = B A \cos \phi$ (for uniform B)
- Faraday's Law
  $\mathcal{E} = -N \frac{d\Phi_B}{dt} = -N \frac{\Delta \Phi_B}{\Delta t}$
- Change in Magnetic Field ($B$)
  $\mathcal{E} = -NA \cos\theta \frac{\Delta B}{\Delta t}$
- Change in Loop Area ($A$)
  $\mathcal{E} = -NB \cos\theta \frac{\Delta A}{\Delta t}$
- Change in Orientation Angle ($\theta$)
  $\mathcal{E} = -NBA \frac{\Delta (\cos\theta)}{\Delta t}$ (Rotation)
- Motional EMF
  $\mathcal{E} = B l v$
- General Form
  If velocity vector makes an angle $\theta$ with the field:
  $\mathcal{E} = B l v \sin \theta$

- Derivation
  $F=qvB$ --$\enclose{circle}{1}$
  $W=F\times l$ --$\enclose{circle}{2}$
  $V=\frac{W}{q}$ --$\enclose{circle}{3}$
  sub $\enclose{circle}{1}$ with --$\enclose{circle}{2}$
  $\frac{W}{l}=qvB$ --$\enclose{circle}{4}$
  sub $\enclose{circle}{4}$ with --$\enclose{circle}{3}$
  $\frac{qV}{l}=qvB$
  $V=Blv$
- Induced EMF in a Rotating Coil
  Consider a rectangular coil of $N$ turns, each of area $A$, rotating with constant angular velocity $\omega$ in a uniform magnetic field $B$.

  $\Phi_B = N B A \cos(\omega t)$
  $\mathcal{E} = -\frac{d\Phi_B}{dt} = -NBA \frac{d}{dt}(\cos \omega t)$
  $\mathcal{E} = NBA\omega \sin(\omega t)$
- Maximum EMF (Peak Voltage)
  Occurs when $\sin(\omega t) = 1$.
  $\mathcal{E}_{max} = \mathcal{E}_0 = NBA\omega$

- Instantaneous EMF
  $\mathcal{E} = \mathcal{E}_0 \sin(\omega t)$

- Transformer Equations
  $\frac{V_S}{V_P} = \frac{N_S}{N_P}$
- Current Ratio (Ideal Transformer)
  Assuming 100% efficiency ($P_{in} = P_{out}$ or $V_P I_P = V_S I_S$):
  $\frac{I_S}{I_P} = \frac{N_P}{N_S}$

- Self-Inductance Formulas
  $\Phi_{L}=LI$
  $\mathcal{E} = -L \frac{dI}{dt}$
  $L = \frac{N\Phi_B}{I}$
- Self-Inductance of a Solenoid
  Derivation: Since $B = \frac{\mu_0 N I}{l}$ and $\Phi = BA$, then $L = \frac{N(BA)}{I}$.
  $L = \frac{\mu_0 N^2 A}{l}$
- Mutual Inductance EMF
  $\mathcal{E}_2 = -M_{21} \frac{dI_1}{dt}$ (EMF in coil 2 due to current in coil 1)
  $\mathcal{E}_1 = -M_{12} \frac{dI_2}{dt}$ (EMF in coil 1 due to current in coil 2)
- Mutual Inductance Definition
  $M_{21} = \frac{N_2 \Phi_{21}}{I_1}$
  $M_{12} = M_{21} = M$
- Mutual inductance for two coaxial solenoids
  $M=\frac{\mu_{0}N_{1}N_{2}A}{l}$
- Energy Stored in an Inductor
  $U = \frac{1}{2} L I^2$
- Energy Density
  $u = \frac{1}{2} \frac{B^2}{\mu_0}$
- Energy stored in solenoid
  $U=\frac{1}{2}\left( \frac{\mu_{0}N^{2}AI^{2}}{l} \right)$

# CHAPTER 10
- Wavelength in a Medium
  $\frac{\lambda_2}{\lambda_1} = \frac{v_2}{v_1} = \frac{n_1}{n_2}$
  $\lambda_n = \frac{\lambda}{n}$
- Condition for Bright Fringes (Constructive Interference)
  Path difference ($\Delta L$) must be an integer number of wavelengths.
  $d \sin \theta = m\lambda$
- Position of Bright Fringes, $y_m$
  For small angles ($\sin \theta \approx \tan \theta \approx y/L$):
  $y_m = \frac{m\lambda L}{d}$
- Condition for Dark Fringes (Destructive Interference)
  $d \sin \theta = (m + \frac{1}{2})\lambda$
- Position of Dark Fringes, $x_m$
  $x_m = (m + \frac{1}{2})\frac{\lambda L}{d}$
- Fringe Separation, $\Delta y$
  $\Delta y = \frac{\lambda L}{d}$
- Condition for Minima (Dark Fringes)
  $a \sin \theta_n = n\lambda$ (or $D \sin \theta = m\lambda$)
- Position of Minima, $y_n$
  If the screen distance $L$ is much larger than the slit width $a$ ($L \gg a$), then $\sin \theta \approx \tan \theta \approx \frac{y_n}{L}$.
  $y_n = \frac{n\lambda L}{a}$
- Condition for Maxima (Bright Fringes)
  $a \sin \theta_n = (n + \frac{1}{2})\lambda$
- Position of Maxima, $y_n$
  If the screen distance $L$ is much larger than the slit width $a$ ($L \gg a$), then $\sin \theta \approx \tan \theta \approx \frac{y_n}{L}$.
  $y_n =\frac{\left( n+\frac{1}{2} \right)\lambda L}{a}$
- Width of Central Maximum, $w$
  The central maximum extends from the first minimum ($n=1$) on one side to the first minimum ($n=-1$) on the other.
  $w = 2y_1 = 2\left( \frac{\lambda L}{a} \right)$
- Angular Width, $\theta_w$
  $\theta_w = 2\theta_1 = 2 \sin^{-1}\left( \frac{\lambda}{a} \right)$

- Slit Separation, $d$
  $d = \frac{1}{N}$
- Condition for Principal Maxima (Bright Fringes)
  $d \sin \theta_n = n\lambda$
- Maximum Number of Orders, $n_{max}$
  The highest order is observed when the diffraction angle is maximum ($\theta = 90^\circ$).
  $d \sin(90^\circ) = n_{max} \lambda \implies n_{max} = \frac{d}{\lambda}$

# CHAPTER 11
- Wien's Displacement Law
  Relates the temperature of a blackbody to the wavelength at which it emits the most intensity ($\lambda_P$).
  $\lambda_P T = 2.90 \times 10^{-3}\,m\cdot K$
- Quantization of Energy
  $E = nhf$
- Energy of a Quantum (Photon)
  $E = hf = \frac{hc}{\lambda}$
- Conversion
  $1\,eV = 1.60 \times 10^{-19}\,J$
- Planck's Radiation Formula
  $I(\lambda, T) = \frac{2\pi h c^2 \lambda^{-5}}{e^{hc/\lambda kT} - 1}$
- Work function
  $W_0 = hf_0 = \frac{hc}{\lambda_0}$
- Einstein's Photoelectric Equation
  $E = W_0 + K_{max}$
  $hf = W_0 + \frac{1}{2}mv_{max}^2$
- Photon Momentum, $p$
  Derived from the relativistic energy-momentum relation ($E^2 = p^2c^2 + m^2c^4$ with $m=0$).
  $p = \frac{E}{c} = \frac{hf}{c} = \frac{h}{\lambda}$
- de Broglie Wavelength, $\lambda$
  ${\overbrace{\textcolor[RGB]{255,255,0}\lambda}^{\text{wave aspect}}}=\frac{h}{\underbrace{\textcolor[RGB]{255,255,0}{p}}_{\text{particle aspect}}}=\frac{h}{mv}$
