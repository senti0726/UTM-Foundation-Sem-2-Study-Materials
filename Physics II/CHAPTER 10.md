# 10.1 Waves versus Particles; Huygens' Principle

> [!definition]- Huygens' Principle
> Every point on a wave front acts as a $\color{orange}i$; the wave front as it develops is $\color{orange}ii$ to all the wavelets.
> > [!blank]- $\color{orange}i$
> > point source
>
> > [!blank]- $\color{orange}ii$
> > tangent

> [!definition]- Wavefront
> A line or surface, in the path of a wave motion, on which the disturbances at every point have the ||same phase||.
> ![[Pasted image 20260203041353.png]]
> > [!continue]- Characteristics
> > * Line joining all points of adjacent waves that are in phase (e.g., crest to crest).
> > * Wavefront is always ||perpendicular|| to the direction of wave propagation.

## Huygens' Principle and the Law of Refraction

> [!continue]- Explanation of Refraction
> Huygens' principle can explain the law of refraction. As wavelets propagate from each point, they propagate $\color{orange}i$ in the medium of $\color{orange}ii$ index of refraction. This leads to a bend in the wave front and therefore in the ray.
> ![[Pasted image 20260203041736.png]]
> > [!blank]- $\color{orange}i$
> > more slowly
>
> > [!blank]- $\color{orange}ii$
> > higher

> [!formula]- Wavelength in a Medium
> The frequency of light does not change, but the wavelength does as it travels into a new medium.
> $\frac{\lambda_2}{\lambda_1} = \frac{v_2}{v_1} = \frac{n_1}{n_2}$
> $\lambda_n = \frac{\lambda}{n}$
> > [!unit]- $\lambda_n$ (or $\lambda_2$)
> > Wavelength in the medium with refractive index $n$ in meters ($m$)
>
> > [!unit]- $\lambda$ (or $\lambda_1$)
> > Wavelength in vacuum (or initial medium) in meters ($m$)
>
> > [!unit]- $n$ (or $n_2$)
> > Refractive index of the medium
>
> > [!unit]- $v_1, v_2$
> > Speed of light in medium 1 and 2 in $m\,s^{-1}$

## Highway Mirage

> [!continue]- Explanation
> Highway mirages are due to a gradually changing $\color{orange}i$ in heated air.
> ![[Pasted image 20260203041901.png]]
> > [!blank]- $\color{orange}i$
> > index of refraction
>
> > [!continue]- Mechanism
> > * On a hot day, the layer of hot air near the roadway is ||less dense|| than the cooler air above.
> > * The refractive index in hot air is slightly ||lower|| than in cooler air.
> > * Light rays approaching the road from the cooler air are bent (refracted) ||away from the normal|| (or upwards) and seem to come from below the road, creating an image as if reflected.

> * The two slits $S_1$ and $S_2$ act as two new sources of $\color{orange}iii$ waves which are in phase.

## Mathematical Analysis

> [!formula]- Condition for Bright Fringes (Constructive Interference)
> ![[Pasted image 20260203090237.png]]
> Path difference ($\Delta L$) must be an integer number of wavelengths.
> $d \sin \theta = m\lambda$
> > [!unit]- $d$
> > Separation between the two slits in meters ($m$)
>
> > [!unit]- $\theta$
> > Angle of diffraction
>
> > [!unit]- $m$
> > $\textcolor[RGB]{255,255,0}{\text{Order}}$ of the interference fringe ($0, \pm 1, \pm 2, \dots$)
>
> > [!unit]- $\lambda$
> > Wavelength of the light in meters ($m$)

> [!formula]- Position of Bright Fringes, $y_m$
> ![[Pasted image 20260203090237.png]]
> For small angles ($\sin \theta \approx \tan \theta \approx y/L$):
> $y_m = \frac{m\lambda L}{d}$
> > [!unit]- $y_m$
> > Distance of the $m$-th bright fringe from the central maximum in meters ($m$)
>
> > [!unit]- $m$
> > Order number ($0, \pm 1, \pm 2, \dots$)
> > * $m=0$: ||Central bright fringe|| (Zeroth order)
> > * $m=\pm 1$: ||First bright fringe|| (First order)
>
> > [!unit]- $\lambda$
> > Wavelength of the light in meters ($m$)
>
> > [!unit]- $L$
> > Distance between double-slits and the screen in meters ($m$)
>
> > [!unit]- $d$
> > Separation between double-slits in meters ($m$)

> [!formula]- Condition for Dark Fringes (Destructive Interference)
> ![[Pasted image 20260203090403.png]]
> Path difference must be a half-integer number of wavelengths.
> $d \sin \theta = (m + \frac{1}{2})\lambda$
> > [!unit]- $d$
> > Separation between slits in meters ($m$)
>
> > [!unit]- $\theta$
> > Angle of diffraction
>
> > [!unit]- $m$
> > Order number ($0, \pm 1, \pm 2, \dots$)
>
> > [!unit]- $\lambda$
> > Wavelength in meters ($m$)

> [!formula]- Position of Dark Fringes, $x_m$
> ![[Pasted image 20260203090409.png]]
> $x_m = (m + \frac{1}{2})\frac{\lambda L}{d}$
> > [!unit]- $x_m$
> > Distance of the $m$-th dark fringe from the central maximum in meters ($m$)
>
> > [!unit]- $m$
> > Order number ($0, \pm 1, \pm 2, \dots$)
> > * $m=0$: ||1st dark fringe|| (Zeroth order minimum)
> > * $m=1$: ||2nd dark fringe|| (1st order minimum)
>
> > [!unit]- $\lambda$
> > Wavelength in meters ($m$)
>
> > [!unit]- $L$
> > Distance to screen in meters ($m$)
>
> > [!unit]- $d$
> > Slit separation in meters ($m$)

> [!formula]- Fringe Separation, $\Delta y$
> The distance between consecutive bright fringes (or consecutive dark fringes).
> $\Delta y = \frac{\lambda L}{d}$
> > [!unit]- $\Delta y$
> > Fringe separation in meters ($m$)
>
> > [!unit]- $\lambda$
> > Wavelength in meters ($m$)
>
> > [!unit]- $L$
> > Distance to screen in meters ($m$)
>
> > [!unit]- $d$
> > Slit separation in meters ($m$)

> [!sq]- Example 2: Comparing wavelengths
> Light of $\lambda_1 = 600\,nm$ produces fringe separation $\Delta y_1 = 7.0\,mm$. Another light source produces $\Delta y_2 = 5.0\,mm$. Calculate $\lambda_2$.
> > [!continue]- Solution
> > **Given:**
> > $\lambda_1 = 600 \times 10^{-9}\,m$
> > $\Delta y_1 = 7.0 \times 10^{-3}\,m$
> > $\Delta y_2 = 5.0 \times 10^{-3}\,m$
> >
> > **Formula:**
> > $\Delta y = \frac{\lambda L}{d} \implies \Delta y \propto \lambda$ (since $L, d$ constant).
> > $\frac{\Delta y_2}{\Delta y_1} = \frac{\lambda_2}{\lambda_1}$
> >
> > **Calculation:**
> > $\lambda_2 = \lambda_1 \left( \frac{\Delta y_2}{\Delta y_1} \right)$
> > $\lambda_2 = 600 \left( \frac{5.0}{7.0} \right)$
> > $\enclose{box}{\lambda_2 \approx 429\,nm}$

> [!sq]- Example 3: Two Slits in Water
> Slit separation $d = 1.2\,mm$, Screen distance $L = 2.5\,m$. Frequency $f = 5.77 \times 10^{14}\,Hz$.
> > [!continue]- $a)$ Wavelength and Separation in Air.
> > **i. Wavelength ($\lambda$):**
> > $c = f\lambda \implies \lambda = \frac{c}{f}$
> > $\lambda = \frac{3.00 \times 10^8}{5.77 \times 10^{14}}$
> > $\enclose{box}{\lambda \approx 5.20 \times 10^{-7}\,m = 520\,nm}$
> >
> > **ii. Fringe Separation ($\Delta y$):**
> > $\Delta y = \frac{\lambda L}{d}$
> > $\Delta y = \frac{(5.20 \times 10^{-7})(2.5)}{1.2 \times 10^{-3}}$
> > $\enclose{box}{\Delta y = 1.08 \times 10^{-3}\,m = 1.08\,mm}$
>
> > [!continue]- $b)$ Separation in Water ($n=1.33$).
> > **New Wavelength ($\lambda_n$):**
> > $\lambda_n = \frac{\lambda}{n} = \frac{5.20 \times 10^{-7}}{1.33} \approx 3.91 \times 10^{-7}\,m$.
> >
> > **New Separation ($\Delta x$):**
> > $\Delta x = \frac{\lambda_n L}{d}$
> > $\Delta x = \frac{(3.91 \times 10^{-7})(2.5)}{1.2 \times 10^{-3}}$
> > $\enclose{box}{\Delta x \approx 8.15 \times 10^{-4}\,m = 0.815\,mm}$

> [!sq]- Example 10-1: Line spacing
> Two slits $0.100\,mm$ apart are $1.20\,m$ from screen. $\lambda = 500\,nm$. How far apart are adjacent bright fringes?
> ![[Pasted image 20260203090832.png]]
> > [!continue]- Solution
> > **Given:**
> > $d = 0.100\,mm = 1.0 \times 10^{-4}\,m$
> > $L = 1.20\,m$
> > $\lambda = 500\,nm = 5.0 \times 10^{-7}\,m$
> >
> > **Calculation:**
> > $\Delta y = \frac{\lambda L}{d}$
> > $\Delta y = \frac{(5.0 \times 10^{-7})(1.20)}{1.0 \times 10^{-4}}$
> > $\enclose{box}{\Delta y = 6.0 \times 10^{-3}\,m = 6.0\,mm}$

> [!note]- Since the position of the maxima (except the central one) depends on
> wavelength, the first- and higher- order fringes contain a spectrum of colours. 

> [!sq]- Example 10-2: Wavelengths from Interference
> White light passes through slits $0.50\,mm$ apart. Screen is $2.5\,m$ away. First order fringe has violet at $2.0\,mm$ and red at $3.5\,mm$ from center. Estimate wavelengths.
> > [!continue]- Solution
> > **Given:**
> > $d = 0.50 \times 10^{-3}\,m$
> > $L = 2.5\,m$
> > $m = 1$
> >
> > **Violet ($\lambda_V$):**
> > $y_V = 2.0\,mm = 2.0 \times 10^{-3}\,m$
> > $y = \frac{m\lambda L}{d} \implies \lambda = \frac{y d}{m L}$
> > $\lambda_V = \frac{(2.0 \times 10^{-3})(0.50 \times 10^{-3})}{(1)(2.5)}$
> > $\enclose{box}{\lambda_V = 4.0 \times 10^{-7}\,m = 400\,nm}$
> >
> > **Red ($\lambda_R$):**
> > $y_R = 3.5\,mm = 3.5 \times 10^{-3}\,m$
> > $\lambda_R = \frac{(3.5 \times 10^{-3})(0.50 \times 10^{-3})}{(1)(2.5)}$
> > $\enclose{box}{\lambda_R = 7.0 \times 10^{-7}\,m = 700\,nm}$

> [!continue]- Single Slit Pattern

## Explanation of Single Slit Diffraction 

> [!continue]- Mechanism
> ![[Pasted image 20260203091927.png]]
> $\theta=$ angle of diffraction
> * Wavefront from light source falls on a narrow slit $S$ and diffraction occurs.
> * Every point on the wavefront acts as a source of $\color{orange}i$ and superposes to form an interference pattern.
> > [!blank]- $\color{orange}i$
> > secondary wavelets
>
> > [!liststart]- Characteristics of the Pattern
> > ![[Pasted image 20260203092227.png]]
> > > [!continue]- Central Fringe
> > > It is ||bright (maximum)|| and ||wider|| compared to other bright fringes.
> > > It has the ||highest intensity||.
> >
> > > [!continue]- Secondary Fringes
> > > The intensity of bright fringes ||reduces|| as the distance increases from the central maximum.

## Mathematical Analysis of Single Slit Diffraction

> [!formula]- Condition for Minima (Dark Fringes)
> The diffraction pattern has minima (dark fringes) when the path difference between top and bottom rays is an integer multiple of wavelengths.
> ![[Pasted image 20260203092941.png]]
> $a \sin \theta_n = n\lambda$ (or $D \sin \theta = m\lambda$)
> > [!unit]- $a$ (or $D$)
> > Width of the single slit in meters ($m$)
>
> > [!unit]- $\theta_n$
> > Angle of diffraction for the $n$-th minimum
>
> > [!unit]- $n$ (or $m$)
> > Order number ($\pm 1, \pm 2, \pm 3, \dots$)
> > * Note: $n \neq 0$ because $n=0$ is the central maximum.
>
> > [!unit]- $\lambda$
> > Wavelength of light in meters ($m$)
> 
> > [!continue]- Explanation
> > > [!continue]- For $2^{nd}$ minimum, AB split into $4$ equal part
> > > ![[Pasted image 20260203093533.png]]
> > > $\frac{a}{4}\sin \theta_{2}=\frac{\lambda}{2}$
> > > $a\sin \theta_{2}=2\lambda$
> > 
> > > [!continue]- For $3^{rd}$ minimum, AB is split in 6 equal part
> > > ![[Pasted image 20260203093655.png]]
> > > $\frac{a}{6}\sin \theta_{3}=\frac{\lambda}{2}$
> > > $a\sin \theta_{3}=3\lambda$

> [!formula]- Position of Minima, $y_n$
> If the screen distance $L$ is much larger than the slit width $a$ ($L \gg a$), then $\sin \theta \approx \tan \theta \approx \frac{y_n}{L}$.
> $y_n = \frac{n\lambda L}{a}$
> > [!unit]- $y_n$
> > Distance of the $n$-the minimum from the central maximum in meters ($m$)
>
> > [!unit]- $n$
> > Order number
> > > [!continue]- When $n=$
> > > > [!continue]- $\pm 1$
> > > > $1^{st}$ minimum fringe
> > > > $1^{st}$ order minimum
> > > 
> > > > [!continue]- $\pm 2$
> > > > $2^{nd}$ minimum fringe
> > > > $2^{nd}$ order minimum
> > > 
> > > > [!continue]- $\pm 3$
> > > > $3^{rd}$ minimum fringe
> > > > $3^{rd}$ order minimum
>
> > [!unit]- $\lambda$
> > Wavelength in meters ($m$)
>
> > [!unit]- $L$
> > Distance from slit to screen in meters ($m$)
>
> > [!unit]- $a$
> > Slit width in meters ($m$)

> [!formula]- Condition for Maxima (Bright Fringes)
> Approximate condition for secondary maxima (excluding central max):
> ![[Pasted image 20260203094117.png]]
> $a \sin \theta_n = (n + \frac{1}{2})\lambda$
> > [!unit]- $a$
> > Slit width in meters ($m$)
>
> > [!unit]- $\theta_n$
> > Angle of diffraction
>
> > [!unit]- $n$
> > Order number ($\pm 1, \pm 2, \dots$)
>
> > [!unit]- $\lambda$
> > Wavelength in meters ($m$)
> 
> > [!continue]- Explanation
> > > [!continue]- For the $2^{nd}$ maximum, AB is split into $5$ equal parts
> > > ![[Pasted image 20260203094220.png]]
> > > $\frac{a}{5}\sin \theta_{2}=\frac{\lambda}{2}$

> > > [!continue]- For the $3^{rd}$ maximum, AB is split into $7$ equal parts

> [!formula]- Position of Maxima, $y_n$
> If the screen distance $L$ is much larger than the slit width $a$ ($L \gg a$), then $\sin \theta \approx \tan \theta \approx \frac{y_n}{L}$.
> $y_n =\frac{\left( n+\frac{1}{2} \right)\lambda L}{a}$
> > [!unit]- $y_n$
> > Distance of the $n$-the maximum from the central maximum in meters ($m$)
>
> > [!unit]- $n$
> > Order number

> [!sq]- Example 10-3: Single-slit diffraction maximum
> Light of wavelength $750\,nm$ passes through a slit $1.0 \times 10^{-3}\,mm$ wide. Screen is $20\,cm$ away.
> ![[Pasted image 20260203095347.png]]
> > [!continue]- How wide is the central maximum?
> > **Given:**
> > $\lambda = 750 \times 10^{-9}\,m$
> > $a = 1.0 \times 10^{-6}\,m$
> > $L = 0.20\,m$
> >
> > **a) Angular Width ($\theta_w$):**
> > First minimum ($n=1$): $\sin \theta_1 = \frac{\lambda}{a} = \frac{750 \times 10^{-9}}{1.0 \times 10^{-6}} = 0.75$
> > $\theta_1 = \sin^{-1}(0.75) \approx 48.6^\circ$
> > $\theta_w = 2\theta_1 = 2(48.6^\circ)$
> > $\enclose{box}{\theta_w \approx 97.2^\circ}$
> >
> > **b) Linear Width ($w$):**
> > Since $\theta$ is large, we cannot use small angle approximation ($w \neq \frac{2\lambda L}{a}$). We must use geometry.
> > $\tan \theta_1 = \frac{y_1}{L}$
> > $y_1 = L \tan \theta_1 = 0.20 \tan(48.6^\circ) \approx 0.20(1.134) \approx 0.227\,m$
> > $w = 2y_1 = 2(0.227)$
> > $\enclose{box}{w \approx 0.45\,m = 45\,cm}$

> [!sq]- Extra Q1: Calculating slit width
> Monochromatic light ($\lambda = 689\,nm$) falls on a slit. Angle between first bright fringes on either side of central maximum is $38^\circ$. Calculate slit width $a$.
> > [!continue]- Solution
> > **Given:**
> > $\lambda = 689 \times 10^{-9}\,m$
> > Angle between 1st maxima $= 38^\circ \implies$ Angle for one side $\theta = 19^\circ$.
> > Order $n = 1$ (for 1st maximum).
> >
> > **Formula:**
> > Maxima condition: $a \sin \theta = (n + \frac{1}{2})\lambda$
> >
> > **Calculation:**
> > $a \sin(19^\circ) = (1 + 0.5)(689 \times 10^{-9})$
> > $a (0.3256) = 1.5(689 \times 10^{-9})$
> > $a = \frac{1.0335 \times 10^{-6}}{0.3256}$
> > $\enclose{box}{a \approx 3.17 \times 10^{-6}\,m}$

> [!sq]- Extra Q2: Distance between dark fringes
> Light ($\lambda = 633\,nm$) incident on slit $a = 0.750\,mm$. Screen distance $L = 3.50\,m$. Determine distance between the two dark fringes on either side of central bright fringe.
> > [!continue]- Solution
> > **Given:**
> > $\lambda = 633 \times 10^{-9}\,m$
> > $a = 0.750 \times 10^{-3}\,m$
> > $L = 3.50\,m$
> >
> > **Concept:**
> > The "two dark fringes on either side of central bright fringe" refers to the 1st minima ($n=1$). The distance between them is the width of the central maximum ($w$).
> >
> > **Calculation:**
> > $w = \frac{2\lambda L}{a}$ (Small angle assumption check: $\lambda/a \approx 10^{-3}$, valid).
> > $w = \frac{2(633 \times 10^{-9})(3.50)}{0.750 \times 10^{-3}}$
> > $w = \frac{4.431 \times 10^{-6}}{0.750 \times 10^{-3}}$
> > $\enclose{box}{w \approx 5.91 \times 10^{-3}\,m = 5.91\,mm}$

> [!sq]- Extra Q3: Second order minima
> Screen $1.00\,m$ behind single slit. Central max is $1.60\,cm$ wide. What is the distance between the two second order minima?
> > [!continue]- Solution
> > **Step 1: Find $a$ from central width**
> > $w = 1.60\,cm = 0.016\,m$.
> > $w = \frac{2\lambda L}{a} \implies \frac{\lambda}{a} = \frac{w}{2L} = \frac{0.016}{2(1)} = 0.008$.
> >
> > **Step 2: Find distance for 2nd minima ($n=2$)**
> > Position $y_2 = \frac{n\lambda L}{a} = \frac{2\lambda L}{a}$.
> > Distance between two 2nd order minima ($w_2$) is $2y_2$.
> > $w_2 = 2 \left( \frac{2\lambda L}{a} \right) = 4 L \left( \frac{\lambda}{a} \right)$.
> >
> > **Calculation:**
> > $w_2 = 4 (1.00) (0.008)$
> > $\enclose{box}{w_2 = 0.032\,m = 3.2\,cm}$

# 10.4 Diffraction Grating

> [!liststart]- Diffraction Grating
> > [!definition]- Diffraction Grating
> a large number of equally spaced parallel slits
> 
> > [!continue]- Construction
> > Made by ruling very fine parallel lines on glass or metal. The spaces between the lines serve as the $\color{orange}i$.
> > > [!blank]- $\color{orange}i$
> > > slits
> > > ![[Pasted image 20260203103843.png]]
> > >  - Light passes through the slit because it is ||transparent||
> > >  - The spaces between the lines are the slits, for example if there are four lines then we have ||3|| slits
>
> > [!liststart]- Types
> > > [!continue]- Transmission Grating
> > > Has slits that allow light to pass through.
> > > > [!example]- 
> > > > usual diffraction grating
> >
> > > [!continue]- Reflection Grating
> > > Has lines that reflect light
> > > > [!example]- 
> > > > CD and DVD
> 
> > [!continue]- Used in
> > spectrometer to determine ||the wavelength of light|| and to study ||spectra||

> [!formula]- Slit Separation, $d$
> If the grating has $N$ lines per unit length (e.g., lines/cm), the slit separation is the inverse of $N$.
> $d = \frac{1}{N}$
> > [!unit]- $d$
> > Separation between adjacent slits in meters ($m$)
>
> > [!unit]- $N$
> > Number of lines per unit length (e.g., $m^{-1}$ or $cm^{-1}$)

## Mathematical Analysis

> [!formula]- Condition for Principal Maxima (Bright Fringes)
> $d \sin \theta_n = n\lambda$
> > [!unit]- $d$
> > Slit separation in meters ($m$)
>
> > [!unit]- $\theta_n$
> > Angle of diffraction for the $n$-th order maximum
>
> > [!unit]- $n$ (or $m$)
> > Order number ($0, \pm 1, \pm 2, \dots$)
> > * $n=0$: ||Zeroth order maximum|| (Central)
> > * $n=1$: ||First order maximum||
>
> > [!unit]- $\lambda$
> > Wavelength of light in meters ($m$)

> [!formula]- Maximum Number of Orders, $n_{max}$
> The highest order is observed when the diffraction angle is maximum ($\theta = 90^\circ$).
> $d \sin(90^\circ) = n_{max} \lambda \implies n_{max} = \frac{d}{\lambda}$
> > [!unit]- $n_{max}$
> > Maximum integer order number possible
>
> > [!unit]- $d$
> > Slit separation in meters ($m$)
>
> > [!unit]- $\lambda$
> > Wavelength in meters ($m$)
>
> > [!info]- Rounding Rule
> > Since $n$ must be an integer, always round **down** to the nearest whole number.

> [!liststart]- Spectra
> ![[Pasted image 20260203110106.png]]
> If white light is used:
> > [!continue]- Central Fringe ($n=0$)
> > Appears ||white|| because all wavelengths interfere constructively at $\theta=0$.
>
> > [!continue]- Higher Orders ($n \neq 0$)
> > Light is dispersed into a ||rainbow spectrum||.
> > * ||Violet|| light ($\lambda \approx 400\,nm$) deviates the least.
> > * ||Red|| light ($\lambda \approx 700\,nm$) deviates the most.
> > * Spectra of different orders may ||overlap||.

## Examples

> [!sq]- Example 7: Unknown Wavelength
> Grating has 3000 lines/cm. Angular separation between first order maxima is $35^\circ$.
> ![[Pasted image 20260203112701.png]]
> > [!continue]- $a)$ Calculate the wavelength $\lambda$.
> > **Given:**
> > $N = 3000\,lines/cm = 3000/0.01\,m = 3 \times 10^5\,lines/m$.
> > $d = \frac{1}{N} = \frac{1}{3 \times 10^5} \approx 3.33 \times 10^{-6}\,m$.
> > Angular separation $2\theta_1 = 35^\circ \implies \theta_1 = 17.5^\circ$.
> > Order $n = 1$.
> >
> > **Calculation:**
> > $d \sin \theta = n\lambda$
> > $(3.33 \times 10^{-6}) \sin(17.5^\circ) = (1)\lambda$
> > $\lambda = (3.33 \times 10^{-6})(0.3007)$
> > $\enclose{box}{\lambda \approx 1.00 \times 10^{-6}\,m}$
>
> > [!continue]- $b)$ Angular separation between 2nd and 3rd order maxima.
> > **Find $\theta_2$ ($n=2$):**
> > $\sin \theta_2 = \frac{2\lambda}{d} = \frac{2(1.00 \times 10^{-6})}{3.33 \times 10^{-6}} = 0.6006$
> > $\theta_2 = \sin^{-1}(0.6006) \approx 36.9^\circ$
> >
> > **Find $\theta_3$ ($n=3$):**
> > $\sin \theta_3 = \frac{3\lambda}{d} = \frac{3(1.00 \times 10^{-6})}{3.33 \times 10^{-6}} = 0.9009$
> > $\theta_3 = \sin^{-1}(0.9009) \approx 64.3^\circ$
> >
> > **Separation:**
> > $\Delta \theta = \theta_3 - \theta_2 = 64.3^\circ - 36.9^\circ$
> > $\enclose{box}{\Delta \theta = 27.4^\circ}$

> [!sq]- Example 10-4: Angular positions
> Grating has 10,000 lines/cm. Find angular positions of 1st and 2nd order maxima for Violet ($400\,nm$) and Red ($700\,nm$).
> > [!continue]- Solution
> > **Given:**
> > $N = 10,000\,lines/cm = 1 \times 10^6\,lines/m$.
> > $d = \frac{1}{N} = 1.0 \times 10^{-6}\,m$.
> >
> > **For Violet ($\lambda = 400\,nm$):**
> > * $n=1$: $\sin \theta = \frac{(1)(400e-9)}{1e-6} = 0.4 \implies \theta = 23.6^\circ$.
> > * $n=2$: $\sin \theta = \frac{(2)(400e-9)}{1e-6} = 0.8 \implies \theta = 53.1^\circ$.
> >
> > **For Red ($\lambda = 700\,nm$):**
> > * $n=1$: $\sin \theta = \frac{(1)(700e-9)}{1e-6} = 0.7 \implies \theta = 44.4^\circ$.
> > * $n=2$: $\sin \theta = \frac{(2)(700e-9)}{1e-6} = 1.4 \implies$ ||Math Error|| (No 2nd order for red).

> [!sq]- Example 10-5: Spectra Overlap
> White light ($400-750\,nm$) on grating (4000 lines/cm). Show that blue ($450\,nm$) of 3rd order overlaps red ($700\,nm$) of 2nd order.
> > [!continue]- Solution
> > **Given:**
> > $N = 4000\,lines/cm \implies d = \frac{1}{400,000} = 2.5 \times 10^{-6}\,m$.
> > Note: Slide calculation uses $d = 4 \times 10^{-5}$? Let's check slide. Slide 68 says $d = 1/4000$? No, unit conversion. $4000/cm = 400,000/m$. $1/400,000 = 2.5 \mu m$.
> > Slide 68 calculation shows $(4 \times 10^{-5})$? Wait, $1/4000 = 0.00025 cm = 2.5 \mu m$.
> > Let's recalculate based on standard physics.
> >
> > **Angle for Blue ($n=3, \lambda=450\,nm$):**
> > $\sin \theta_B = \frac{3(450 \times 10^{-9})}{d}$.
> >
> > **Angle for Red ($n=2, \lambda=700\,nm$):**
> > $\sin \theta_R = \frac{2(700 \times 10^{-9})}{d}$.
> >
> > **Comparison:**
> > $3 \times 450 = 1350\,nm$.
> > $2 \times 700 = 1400\,nm$.
> > Since $1350 \approx 1400$, the angles are very close.
> > $\theta_B$ comes from $\sin^{-1}(1350e-9/d)$.
> > $\theta_R$ comes from $\sin^{-1}(1400e-9/d)$.
> > The 3rd order blue is actually at a *smaller* angle than 2nd order red ($1350 < 1400$).
> > This means the 3rd order spectrum *begins* inside the 2nd order spectrum. Thus, they overlap.

> [!table]- Comparison: Interference vs Diffraction
> | Aspect | Diffraction (Single Slit) | Interference (Double Slit) |
> | :--- | :--- | :--- |
> | **Intensity** | Secondary maxima have unequal intensity (decreasing). | All bright bands have roughly equal intensity. |
> | **Width** | Central maximum is wider (2x) than others. | All fringes have ||equal|| width. |
> | **Minima** | Intensity is not completely zero. | Intensity of dark fringes is ||completely zero||. |

> > > [!continue]- Diffraction
> > > intensity of all the secondary maxima is not equal
> > 
> > > [!continue]- Interference
> > > intensity of all the bright bands are equal
> 
> > [!continue]- Width
> > > [!continue]- Diffraction
> > > The width of the central maxima is large
> > 
> > > [!continue]- Interference
> > > The width of all the interference fringes are equal

> > > The intensity of secondary minima is minimal (not completely zero)
